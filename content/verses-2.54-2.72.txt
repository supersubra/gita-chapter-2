# Part 5: The steady mind (2.54–2.72)
intro: Arjuna asks what a steady person is like. Krishna describes someone who is content inside, careful about what the senses take in, and at peace.

## 2.54
who: Arjuna speaking
sk: स्थितप्रज्ञस्य का भाषा समाधिस्थस्य केशव । स्थितधीः किं प्रभाषेत किमासीत व्रजेत किम् ॥
read: sthita-prajnasya kaa bhaashaa samaadhi-sthasya keshava | sthita-dheeh kim prabhaasheta kim aaseeta vrajeta kim
t: What does a steady person look like?
one: Arjuna asks a practical question: how does a steady person speak, sit and move?
meaning: "Krishna, what is a person of steady wisdom like? How does such a person speak, how do they sit, and how do they walk?"
gem: My steady Self (learning from steady people)
star: Arjuna's question opens the most loved part of this chapter: the picture of the sthitaprajna, the person of steady wisdom. The eighteen verses that answer it are among the most recited in the whole Gita.
next: Krishna will answer in the next eighteen verses, describing a steady person one quality at a time.
s: The calm classmate | One girl in your class never panics before tests and never gossips. You and your friends call her "the monk." One day you ask her seriously, "How do you stay like that?" She is surprised that anyone noticed. "I do three small things," she says. "I finish my revision the day before, so there is nothing left to panic about in the morning. I do not discuss the paper at the gate, before or after. And when people start talking about someone who is not there, I go and fill my water bottle." You try the first one that same week.
s: The boy everyone goes to | An older boy in your colony is the one everyone goes to in a crisis. You start watching how he behaves: how he listens, how he answers slowly, how he never talks about people behind their backs.
s: Your mother on a bad morning | The tank overflows, the guests arrive early and the gas runs out, all in one hour, and your mother stays level. You ask your father, "Was she always like this?" He says, "Watch what she does each morning. She takes ten quiet minutes before anyone else is awake."
h: **Find one steady person** among your friends, family or teachers.
h: **Watch what they actually do,** in how they speak, wait and react.
h: **Ask them directly.** Most steady people are happy to share how they learned it.
try: Name the steadiest person you know, and write three things they do differently.
bhakti: Arjuna asks to see wisdom in a living person. God often teaches us through the steady people He places around us.

## 2.55
who: Krishna speaking
sk: प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान् । आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ॥
read: prajahaati yadaa kaamaan sarvaan paartha mano-gataan | aatmany evaatmanaa tushtah sthita-prajnas tadochyate
t: Content within
one: A steady person is content inside and does not need a long list of things to feel happy.
meaning: "Arjuna, when a person gives up all the cravings of the mind and is content in the Self alone, that person is said to be of steady wisdom."
gem: Evenness (contentment)
s: The birthday without a party | Your friend's family cannot afford a party this year. She invites three of you home for her mother's lemon rice and a board game. She is so plainly happy that you all agree it is the best birthday of the year.
s: The friend who does not need the plan | Your group's weekend trip is cancelled, and everyone is sulking. One friend shrugs, picks up a football and says, "Ground at five?" His happiness never depended on the trip.
s: The same old watch | Your cousins are always getting new phones, bags and shoes. Your grandfather has had the same watch and the same few shirts for years, and he seems the most at ease of all of you. "I have enough," he says, and he means it.
h: **Notice what you already have** before you look at what is missing.
h: **Enjoy simple time with friends,** with no spending and no plan.
h: **When a want appears, ask,** "Will I be all right without this?" The answer is usually yes.
try: Spend one evening this week with a friend doing something that costs nothing.
note: **When someone has what you do not.** Jealousy begins with counting what another person has and you lack. Contentment begins with counting differently, and with practice, what others have stops deciding how you feel. These steps help:
ni: **Admit the feeling to yourself.** Almost everyone feels jealous at times. Seeing it clearly is the first step to letting it go.
ni: **Remember that you see only one part of their life.** You see the new phone or the prize. You do not see their worries.
ni: **Count what you already have.** Each night, name three things you would not exchange for anything: a person, a skill, a place or a memory.
ni: **Say something glad out loud.** "That is lovely. I am happy for you." The words help the heart to follow.
ni: **Remember that turns come at different times.** Today the good thing has come to them. On other days it has come to you, and it will again.
ni: **Ask for what you need, not for what they have.** "I need shoes that fit" is a need. "I need the shoes she has" is only comparison.
bhakti: The one who is content within has found the Lord seated there. No other treasure is needed.

## 2.56
sk: दुःखेष्वनुद्विग्नमनाः सुखेषु विगतस्पृहः । वीतरागभयक्रोधः स्थितधीर्मुनिरुच्यते ॥
read: duhkheshv anudvigna-manaah sukheshu vigata-sprihah | veeta-raaga-bhaya-krodhah sthita-dheer munir uchyate
t: Free of clinging, fear and anger
one: A steady person is not shaken in sorrow, does not chase pleasure, and is free of clinging, fear and anger.
meaning: "One whose mind is not shaken in sorrow, who does not crave pleasure, and who is free from attachment, fear and anger, is called a sage of steady mind."
gem: Evenness
s: The quarrel that did not happen | A friend accuses you, in front of others, of spoiling the group's chart. Your face goes hot. You say, "Let us look at it after the period." By then she has cooled down, you find the real mistake together, and the friendship is unharmed.
s: Fear of being left out | Your group is going to a late-night party you are not comfortable with. You are afraid that if you say no, you will be dropped. You say, "Not this one. Breakfast tomorrow?" They tease you for a minute, and they come to breakfast.
s: The angry message | A friend sends a rude message in the group. You type a sharp reply, then delete it. You call him instead. He has had a terrible day at home and apologises within a minute.
h: **Put time between the feeling and the reply.** Wait an hour, or a night.
h: **Name what you feel.** "I am angry" or "I am afraid" is easier to manage than a storm with no name.
h: **Talk in person** when a message has upset you.
h: **When someone criticises you, listen first.** Stay calm, do not defend yourself straight away, and look for the part that is true. A correction taken with an open mind makes you better. A meltdown teaches you nothing.
try: The next time you feel angry, count ten slow breaths before you say or type anything.
bhakti: Clinging, fear and anger leave a heart that trusts God. Each time we pause instead of reacting, we make room for that trust.

## 2.57
sk: यः सर्वत्रानभिस्नेहस्तत्तत्प्राप्य शुभाशुभम् । नाभिनन्दति न द्वेष्टि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥
read: yah sarvatraanabhisnehas tat tat praapya shubhaashubham | naabhinandati na dveshti tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa
t: Neither carried away nor bitter
one: A steady person is not carried away when good things come and does not turn bitter when bad things come.
meaning: "One who is unattached everywhere, who neither rejoices on meeting good fortune nor hates bad fortune, has steady wisdom."
gem: Evenness (the same in good luck and bad)
s: The window seat | On the train to your grandparents' town, you get the window seat. You are thrilled, and you tease your brother about it for the whole journey. On the way back, he gets the window and you get the middle seat, and you sulk for six hours. Your grandmother is travelling with you. Whichever seat she gets, she is the same. She looks out when there is a window and tells stories when there is not. "A seat is only a seat," she says. "The journey is what we came for."
s: Her new cycle, your old one | Your sister gets a brand-new cycle because her new school is far away. Yours is three years old. You turn bitter: "They always give her more." Your father hears you and says, "Last year the new thing in this house was your keyboard. Nobody counted then. We give each of you what you need when you need it. Fair does not always mean the same thing on the same day." You think back and find he is right. You had never counted the times when it was your turn.
s: The broken arm | Two weeks before the colony tournament you have trained for all year, you fall and break your arm. For three days you are bitter with everyone, and you keep asking, "Why me?" Then the coach asks if you will keep the scores and time the practice runs. You say yes. On the day of the tournament you are on the ground with a whistle and a plaster, shouting for your team. You did not get the day you wanted, but you did not waste the day you got.
h: **Enjoy good things without showing off or holding on tight.** They came, and they will change.
h: **When something goes badly, say, "This is what has come. What can I do now?"** Asking that question is how people bounce back.
h: **Do not keep a count of what a brother, sister or friend received.** Parents give each child what that child needs at the time. Fair does not always mean equal on the same day.
h: **If something still feels unfair, say so calmly to your parent, once,** and listen to the answer.
try: The next time a small thing does not go your way, say "All right" and move on without complaining to anyone.
bhakti: The same Lord gives to each of us in His own time. Receiving the sweet and the bitter with the same folded hands is a way of trusting Him.

## 2.58
sk: यदा संहरते चायं कूर्मोऽङ्गानीव सर्वशः । इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥
read: yadaa samharate chaayam koormo 'ngaaneeva sarvashah | indriyaaneendriyaarthebhyas tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa
t: Like a tortoise
one: Like a tortoise pulling in its limbs, a steady person can draw the senses back whenever needed.
meaning: "When a person draws the senses back from their objects, as a tortoise draws in its limbs, that person's wisdom is steady."
gem: Mindful action (choosing what my senses take in)
s: The talk in the next room | Two of your aunts are talking in low voices in the next room about your cousin's trouble at home. You could stand near the door and hear everything, and you badly want to know. Then you think, "This was not meant for my ears." You go out to the balcony and water the plants. Later your cousin tells you about it himself, in his own words, and you are glad you can look him in the eye.
s: Straight to the shop | You go to the mall with your birthday money to buy a shawl for your grandmother. Every window calls to your eyes: shoes, headphones, a watch, a game. Each one says, "Just come in and look." You know that if you look, you will want, and if you want, the money will go. So you keep your eyes on the way ahead, as a tortoise keeps its limbs in, and walk straight to the shawl shop. On the way out, with the shawl in your hand, you can look at the windows as much as you like. They have nothing to pull at now.
s: The warm blanket | You have promised to go running with your father at six. The alarm rings, the morning is cold, and the blanket is warm and soft. Your whole body says, "Five more minutes." You know those five minutes will become an hour. You put your feet on the cold floor before your mind can argue. Ten minutes later you are outside in the fresh air, and you are glad that you made the decision and the blanket did not.
h: **Know your five gates.** Eyes, ears, tongue, nose and skin each pull you towards what they like. Notice which one pulls you most.
h: **Pull in early.** It is easier not to look, listen or taste the first time than to stop halfway.
h: **Pulling in is not hiding.** The tortoise puts its limbs out again when it chooses. You are learning to choose too.
try: Choose one sense today. When it pulls you towards something you do not need, such as a second look, a conversation that is not yours or five more minutes in bed, draw it back once, on purpose.
bhakti: The tortoise is not afraid of the world. It simply knows when to turn inward. Prayer is our way of turning inward to God.

## 2.59
sk: विषया विनिवर्तन्ते निराहारस्य देहिनः । रसवर्जं रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते ॥
read: vishayaa vinivartante niraahaarasya dehinah | rasa-varjam raso 'py asya param drishtvaa nivartate
t: The taste remains
one: Staying away from something removes the thing but not the taste for it. The taste fades when you find something better.
meaning: "The objects of the senses fall away from one who does not feed on them, but the taste for them remains. Even the taste falls away when one has seen the Highest."
gem: Mindful action (replacing a habit with something better)
s: The deleted app | You delete a game before exams, and for a week you think about it all the time. Then your friend drags you to the ground for football every evening. After a month you realise you have not thought about the game for days.
s: Giving up junk food | You and your brother swear off fried snacks and spend every evening talking about fried snacks. Then you both learn to make a decent sandwich, and the craving becomes much quieter.
s: The scrolling habit | You set a limit on your social apps and keep overriding it. What finally works is joining a friend's evening badminton group. When your evenings are full of something you enjoy, you forget to scroll.
h: **Do not only remove a habit. Replace it** with something you enjoy more.
h: **Expect the craving to stay for a while.** That is normal and not a failure.
h: **Find a friend to share the better thing with.**
try: Choose one habit you want to reduce, and write what you will do instead at that time of day.
bhakti: The higher taste Krishna speaks of is the joy of knowing God. Once we have tasted a little of it, smaller cravings loosen by themselves.

## 2.60
sk: यततो ह्यपि कौन्तेय पुरुषस्य विपश्चितः । इन्द्रियाणि प्रमाथीनि हरन्ति प्रसभं मनः ॥
read: yatato hy api kaunteya purushasya vipashchitah | indriyaani pramaatheeni haranti prasabham manah
t: Even the wise can slip
one: The senses are strong and can carry off even a wise person who is trying hard, so do not be overconfident.
meaning: "Arjuna, the restless senses can forcibly carry away the mind of even a wise person who is striving."
gem: Mindful action (being honest about temptation)
s: "Just one level" | You are the careful one at home. You always finish your homework before you play, and your parents trust you with the tablet. One Sunday a cousin shows you a new game, and you tell yourself, "Just one level." When you look up, three hours have gone, lunch is cold and your homework is untouched. You are shocked at yourself. You go to your mother and tell her honestly what happened. "From tomorrow, please keep the tablet with you until my homework is done," you say. She smiles. "Being careful does not mean you will never slip. It means you know what to do when you have."
s: The friend who "would never" | Your friend always said he would never copy. In a tough internal exam, an open book is lying within reach, and he glances at it. Afterwards he tells you, ashamed. You do not judge him. You sit apart from the books together next time.
s: The festival sweets | You have decided to cut down on sweets. Then the festival boxes arrive, and by evening you have finished half a box on your own. The next morning you ask your mother to keep the boxes in her cupboard and give you two a day.
h: **Do not say, "I am strong enough."** Keep temptation out of reach instead.
h: **Tell a parent or a friend where you are weak,** and let them help.
h: **If you slip, do not hide it.** Admit it, repair it and start again.
h: **Keep your gadgets safe.** Lock your phone or tablet, and never share your password, even with a close friend. When you need help with a temptation, leave the device itself with a parent for a while.
try: Put one thing that tempts you out of easy reach, for example by logging out of it or leaving it with a parent.
bhakti: Krishna is frank that this is hard, even for the wise. That is why He asks us, in the next verse, to lean on Him.

## 2.61
sk: तानि सर्वाणि संयम्य युक्त आसीत मत्परः । वशे हि यस्येन्द्रियाणि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥
read: taani sarvaani samyamya yukta aaseeta mat-parah | vashe hi yasyendriyaani tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa
t: Lean on Me
one: Hold the senses steady and fix your mind on God. With His support, self-control becomes possible.
meaning: "Having brought all the senses under control, one should sit steady, with Me as the highest goal. One whose senses are under control has steady wisdom."
gem: My steady Self (an anchor in God)
s: The lamp at dusk | Every evening at your friend's house, her grandmother lights the lamp in the pooja room. Your friend stops whatever she is doing, washes her hands and feet, stands before the deity and chants the Hanuman Chalisa. You used to wait outside and wish she would hurry. One evening you stand beside her instead. It takes only a few minutes. You notice how the whole house goes quiet, and how calm she is when she comes out to play again.
s: Calm on a tense morning | Your elder brother has done his sandhyavandana every morning and evening since his upanayanam. On the morning of his biggest exam, the whole house is tense, and your mother is checking his hall ticket for the third time. He does his sandhyavandana as on any other day and comes out the calmest person in the house. You ask him how. "For those few minutes my mind is with God and with nothing else," he says. "Whatever happens in the exam hall, I have already stood before Him today."
s: One shloka before the phone | You begin each day by checking messages in bed, and you are tense before you are even up. Your mother suggests something else: wash your face, go to the pooja room, fold your hands before the deity and chant one small shloka you know. You try it for a week, and the mornings feel like your own again. Then one day, just before you go on stage, your heart is pounding, and the same shloka comes to your lips by itself. The calm of the pooja room comes with it.
h: **Give the mind something higher to hold.** Willpower alone gets tired.
h: **Keep one small daily practice at a fixed time.** It may be a shloka before the deity in the morning, your sandhyavandana, or lighting the lamp and chanting the Hanuman Chalisa in the evening.
h: **Do it every day, not only on difficult days.** A daily practice is like an anchor. An anchored boat still feels the waves, but it does not drift away. Because you have remembered God that morning, you remember Him in the tense moment too, and you stay calmer than you otherwise would.
h: **Stay close to friends whose habits steady you.**
try: Before you touch your phone tomorrow morning, wash your face, stand before the deity at home with folded hands and chant one shloka you know.
bhakti: Here Krishna says "Me" for the first time in the chapter. Self-control is not a lonely fight. He is the support.

## 2.62
sk: ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते । सङ्गात्सञ्जायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥
read: dhyaayato vishayaan pumsah sangas teshoopajaayate | sangaat sanjaayate kaamah kaamaat krodho 'bhijaayate
t: How it begins
one: It begins with thinking about something again and again. That grows into attachment, then craving, then anger.
meaning: "When a person keeps thinking about the objects of the senses, attachment to them grows. From attachment comes craving, and from craving comes anger."
gem: Mindful action (catching it early)
s: The shoes | Your friend shows you the shoes he wants. You look at them online every night. Soon you feel you must have them. When your parents say, "Not this month," you slam your door. Later you wonder how a pair of shoes made you shout at your mother.
s: Checking her profile | A classmate posts photos every weekend of outings and birthday parties with her group. You begin looking at her profile every night. At first you are only curious. After a week, you start to feel that you belong in those photos. After two weeks, you want very badly to be invited. The invitation does not come, and one morning you find you are cold and sharp with her, though she has done nothing to you. She has no idea why. Trace it back and you will find every step of this verse: looking again and again, then attachment, then craving, then anger.
s: The gift you kept imagining | Your grandfather is bringing a new cycle, and for weeks you imagine yourself riding it. When it turns out to be for your brother, you are furious with him, though he did nothing wrong. You trace the anger back and find that it began with the daydream.
h: **Notice what you keep going back to** in your thoughts and on your screen.
h: **Stop at the first step.** It is far easier to stop looking than to stop being angry.
h: **When you are angry, ask,** "What did I want that I did not get?"
try: Notice one thing you look at again and again, and skip it for one day.
bhakti: What we dwell on shapes us, and that works both ways. The mind grows fond of whatever it visits often, so give it good places to visit. Keep a picture of your favourite deity where you study, and look at it for a moment before you begin. Chant God's name or a small shloka while you walk, wait or lie down to sleep. Listen to one story of Krishna, Rama or Hanuman every week. Thinking of God often grows into fondness, fondness grows into love, and that love brings peace where the other ladder brought anger.

## 2.63
sk: क्रोधाद्भवति सम्मोहः सम्मोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः । स्मृतिभ्रंशाद् बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति ॥
read: krodhaad bhavati sammohah sammohaat smriti-vibhramah | smriti-bhramshaad buddhi-naasho buddhi-naashaat pranashyati
t: Where anger leads
one: Anger clouds the mind, the clouded mind forgets what it knows, and then good sense is lost.
meaning: "From anger comes confusion, from confusion loss of memory, and from loss of memory the ruin of understanding. When understanding is ruined, the person is lost."
gem: Evenness (stopping the slide)
s: The broken model | You have spent two weeks building a model for the science fair. Your little brother picks it up while you are out, and it slips and breaks. Follow what happens next, one step at a time. || **Anger.** You see the pieces on the floor, and your face goes hot. || **Confusion.** In that heat you can no longer see things at their true size. A broken model looks like the end of the world, and your brother looks like an enemy. || **Memory is lost.** You forget things you know perfectly well. He is only seven. He picked it up because he admires everything you make. He sat by your bed with his own toys when you had fever last month. You have broken his things too. All of this is true, and none of it is in your mind. || **Good sense is lost.** With all that forgotten, nothing is left to guide you. You shout, "I wish I did not have a brother," and you snatch his drawing book off the table and tear it. || **Ruin.** Now two things are broken, not one. He is crying, your mother is upset, and the fair is still tomorrow. An hour later everything you forgot comes back, and you feel worse than he does. The model took one evening to mend. His trust in you took much longer. || The slide can be stopped only near the top. If you had walked out of the room at the first step, only a model would have been broken.
h: **Do nothing important while angry.** Do not speak, send or decide.
h: **Leave the room, drink water, walk.** Understanding returns when the heat goes down.
h: **Repair quickly.** Say sorry the same day if you can.
try: Make a rule with a friend that either of you can say "pause" in a quarrel, and the other will stop.
bhakti: Anger makes us forget who we are and who is in front of us. Saying God's name in that moment helps us remember both.

## 2.64
sk: रागद्वेषवियुक्तैस्तु विषयानिन्द्रियैश्चरन् । आत्मवश्यैर्विधेयात्मा प्रसादमधिगच्छति ॥
read: raaga-dvesha-viyuktais tu vishayaan indriyaish charan | aatma-vashyair vidheyaatmaa prasaadam adhigachchhati
t: Using things without being used
one: You can live among all the things of the world and stay peaceful, if strong likes and dislikes do not rule you.
meaning: "But one who moves among the objects of the senses with self-control, free from attraction and dislike, attains peace."
gem: Evenness (enjoying without being hooked)
s: The friend who can stop | You and your friend both enjoy the same game. He plays for half an hour and stops. You cannot. He is not stricter than you. He says, "I like it. I just like other things too."
s: The last gulab jamun | Your mother has made gulab jamun, and you have been thinking about your share all through practice. You come home to an empty bowl. Your brother has eaten the last two, and one of them was yours. Your first feeling is fury: "He always takes more!" Then you catch yourself. It is a sweet. You have enjoyed it a hundred times, and you will again. You say, "You owe me one next time," and laugh. Your brother was ready for a fight. He is so surprised that he breaks his chocolate in half and gives you the bigger piece. You find that you can love a sweet and still be at peace without it.
s: The match on television | You love watching cricket, and so does your uncle. He sits down for the last ten overs, enjoys every ball and switches off. You learn his method: decide how much you will watch before you sit down.
h: **Enjoy things, and keep the off-switch in your own hand.**
h: **Decide the limit before you start,** not in the middle.
h: **Do not hate the things you are avoiding.** Hating keeps you tied to them as well.
try: Enjoy one treat today, in a fixed amount or for a fixed time, and stop when you said you would.
bhakti: Krishna's word for the peace that follows is prasada, the same word we use for food blessed by God. Peace is His gift to a mind that is not grasping.

## 2.65
sk: प्रसादे सर्वदुःखानां हानिरस्योपजायते । प्रसन्नचेतसो ह्याशु बुद्धिः पर्यवतिष्ठते ॥
read: prasaade sarva-duhkhaanaam haanir asyopajaayate | prasanna-chetaso hy aashu buddhih paryavatishthate
t: What peace does
one: In a peaceful mind, sorrows fade and clear thinking returns quickly.
meaning: "In that peace, all sorrows come to an end. The understanding of a person with a peaceful mind soon becomes steady."
gem: Evenness (peace first, then answers)
s: The missed bus | You wake up late on a day that matters, and the bus is already gone. You are furious with yourself, and your eyes are stinging. An elderly neighbour sees your face and says, "Sit down for a minute and drink some water." After a few slow breaths, your mind stops racing. You remember that your cousin lives two streets away and goes the same way. You call, and you arrive only a little late, calm enough to do your best.
s: The joke at the family gathering | At a family gathering, a relative compares you with a cousin in front of everyone and makes a joke about your marks. Your face burns, and the words you want to say are not kind. You step outside instead and breathe. Your sister comes out and sits beside you without a word. After a few quiet minutes, the hurt shrinks, and you can think clearly about what to say. You return and answer politely, and later you tell a parent how it made you feel.
s: The missing wallet | At the bus stop, you open your bag and find your wallet missing, with your ID card, some cash and a few photos you love. Your heart pounds, and you start blaming yourself and everyone else. A shopkeeper nearby asks you to sit and brings you a glass of water. After a few slow breaths, you begin to think step by step: where did you last use it? Within minutes you remember leaving it at the stationery shop, and when you walk back, it is safe on the counter.
h: **When you are upset, sit down, drink some water and breathe slowly.** Do this first, before you try to solve anything.
h: **Let a calm person help you.** A neighbour, a sister or a shopkeeper who says "Sit for a minute" is giving you exactly what you need.
h: **Then think step by step.** Once the mind is quiet, the answer is often already there.
h: **Do not trust the thoughts of an upset mind** late at night.
try: The next time you are upset, sit down, drink a glass of water and take ten slow breaths before you do anything else.
bhakti: Peace is not the prize at the end. It is the ground on which everything else can be done. We can ask God for it first.

## 2.66
sk: नास्ति बुद्धिरयुक्तस्य न चायुक्तस्य भावना । न चाभावयतः शान्तिरशान्तस्य कुतः सुखम् ॥
read: naasti buddhir ayuktasya na chaayuktasya bhaavanaa | na chaabhaavayatah shaantir ashaantasya kutah sukham
t: No peace, no happiness
one: Without a steady mind there is no peace, and without peace there can be no happiness.
meaning: "The unsteady person has no understanding and no power to reflect. Without reflection there is no peace, and without peace, how can there be happiness?"
gem: Evenness
s: The birthday that had everything | It is your birthday, and you have everything you asked for: the gift you wanted, your favourite food, your friends and a cake. Now follow your mind through the day. || **An unsteady mind.** It never stays on one thing. While you open a gift, you are checking your phone to see who has wished you. While your friends sing, you are comparing your gift with the one your cousin got last month. While you eat, you are wondering what comes next. || **No understanding.** A mind that does not stay cannot see what is in front of it. You do not notice that your grandmother travelled two hours to be there, or that your friend made her card by hand. || **No reflection.** You never pause long enough to let any of it sink in, so you never feel thankful. || **No peace.** By evening you are restless and cross, and you snap at your sister over nothing. || **No happiness.** At night you lie awake thinking, "I had everything today. Why was I not happy?" || The next morning you sit quietly with the handmade card and read it slowly. You call your grandmother to thank her. The happiness that a whole day of good things could not give you arrives in ten quiet minutes.
h: **When you feel dull or irritated, look for what has disturbed your peace.**
h: **Build some quiet into each day,** even ten minutes.
h: **Choose company and activities that leave you calmer,** not more restless.
try: Tonight, write one thing from today that left you peaceful and one that left you restless.
bhakti: Krishna's question, "How can there be happiness without peace?", is worth carrying with us. Peace comes from keeping the mind with Him.

## 2.67
sk: इन्द्रियाणां हि चरतां यन्मनोऽनुविधीयते । तदस्य हरति प्रज्ञां वायुर्नावमिवाम्भसि ॥
read: indriyaanaam hi charataam yan mano 'nuvidheeyate | tad asya harati prajnaam vaayur naavam ivaambhasi
t: A boat in the wind
one: A mind that follows every wandering sense is carried off course, like a boat in the wind.
meaning: "When the mind runs after the wandering senses, it carries away a person's understanding, as the wind carries away a boat on the water."
gem: Mindful action (holding the rudder)
s: The promise and the playground | You have promised your grandfather that you will be home by five to walk with him to the temple. On the way back, you hear shouting from the ground. A match is on. "Just two overs," you tell yourself. Your ears were pulled first, then your eyes. Your mind followed them, and it took your promise along with it. When you look up, it is dark. He is still sitting by the gate with his walking stick, and he says nothing. You did not stop loving him at any point, and you never decided to break your word. You were a boat with nobody holding the rudder.
s: The story that went too far | At dinner with your cousins, you begin telling a funny story about a friend. Everyone laughs, and the laughter feels good. So you add a little more, then you exaggerate, and then, to keep them laughing, you tell them something she had told only you. Nobody forced you, and you never meant to give away her secret. The pleasure of being listened to was the wind, and it carried you one small step at a time. That night you cannot sleep. The next day you tell her yourself and say sorry.
s: The same wind, a steady hand | Your sister made the same promise to your grandfather last week. She heard the same match from the same ground, and she loves cricket more than you do. She waved, shouted, "Tomorrow!" and kept walking. The wind did not blow any more gently for her. She had her hand on the rudder. She had decided where she was going before she set out, and so one pleasant sound could not turn her.
h: **Decide where you are going before you set out.** A boat with a rudder can use the wind. A boat without one is used by it.
h: **Watch the first small pull.** It never says, "Break your promise." It says, "Just two minutes."
h: **When you notice you have drifted, come back at once,** without scolding yourself, and put right what you can.
try: Before one task today, tell yourself where you are going: "For the next half hour, I am doing only this." Each time something pulls at you, notice it and come back.
bhakti: A boat needs a rudder and a steady hand. When we give the rudder of the mind to God, the winds lose their power over us.

## 2.68
sk: तस्माद्यस्य महाबाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः । इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥
read: tasmaad yasya mahaa-baaho nigriheetaani sarvashah | indriyaaneendriyaarthebhyas tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa
t: Master of your senses
one: So the person whose senses are fully in hand is the one whose wisdom is steady.
meaning: "Therefore, mighty Arjuna, one whose senses are completely held back from their objects has steady wisdom."
gem: Mindful action (self-control as strength)
s: The friend who says no easily | When your group pushes someone to try a cigarette behind the school, one friend just says, "No, I'm fine," and changes the subject. Nobody teases him for long. You see that his "no" is a kind of strength, and you stand next to him.
s: The exam month | Your cousin goes to college from home, and her exams are a month away. Home is comfortable, and that is the difficulty: the television in the hall, her phone, her bed two steps from her desk, snacks in the kitchen and someone always ready to chat. Nobody sets rules for a college student, so she sets her own and tells the family. Her phone stays with her mother during her study hours. She studies at the desk and never on the bed. She watches television with everyone for half an hour after dinner, takes a walk on the terrace at sunset and sleeps by half past ten. When friends message about a film, she says, "After the twentieth." Her family lowers the television without being asked. After the results, her younger brother asks her to write a timetable for him too.
s: Movies till morning | At a sleepover, your cousins want to watch movies till morning and press you when you refuse. You keep saying, cheerfully, "I am in for one, and then I sleep." In time they stop pressing, and one cousin quietly rolls out her mat next to yours.
h: **Practise saying no in a friendly voice,** without a long explanation.
h: **Set your rule in advance,** so that you are not deciding under pressure.
h: **Stand beside a friend who says no.** It makes it easier for both of you.
try: Say a calm, friendly "no" to one small thing today that you do not really want to do.
bhakti: Self-control is not a cage. It is the strength that lets us offer a whole, undivided mind to God.

## 2.69
sk: या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी । यस्यां जाग्रति भूतानि सा निशा पश्यतो मुनेः ॥
read: yaa nishaa sarva-bhootaanaam tasyaam jaagarti samyamee | yasyaam jaagrati bhootaani saa nishaa pashyato muneh
t: Awake to different things
one: A wise person is awake to what others ignore, and is not excited by what excites everyone else.
meaning: "What is like night to most people is where the self-controlled person is wide awake: the knowledge and joy of the Self. And what most people are wide awake to, the passing pleasures of the world, is like night to the wise person who sees." Here, night stands for what a person does not notice or care for, and being awake stands for what they give their attention to.
gem: My steady Self (my own values)
s: Not interested in the gossip | At lunch, your whole table is excited about who said what about whom. One friend eats quietly and then asks you how your grandmother's operation went. You realise what she pays attention to, and what she does not.
s: Carnatic music and film songs | You have learned Carnatic music since you were small, and you are good at it. Hardly anyone in your class knows what it is. Everyone sings film songs, which have a huge following, and you start hiding your music class as if it were something uncool. Then you sing at a school function. The hall goes quiet, and afterwards the same friends ask, "How do you do that?" You see that what you are good at does not have to be the popular thing. Growing it is what makes you stand out.
s: The leaked paper | The night before the maths exam, a photo of the question paper goes round the class group. Everyone is wide awake and excited, passing it on and solving it together. Your friend does not open it. "I want to know what I actually know," she says, and she goes to sleep at ten. Some of the others think she is foolish. In the morning she writes her paper with a quiet mind. What kept the whole group awake did not interest her at all. What she was awake to, being honest when nobody would have known, was something the others had not even noticed.
h: **Ask what you value,** not only what everyone around you is excited about.
h: **It is all right to be the only one** who is not interested in something, or the only one who stays honest when everyone else takes a shortcut.
h: **Give your best hours** to what matters most to you.
h: **Know what you are good at and keep developing it,** even when it is not the cool thing.
try: Notice one thing "everyone" is excited about that you do not really care for, and let yourself skip it.
bhakti: The devotee is awake to God's presence, which the busy world sleeps through. The quiet of early morning is a good time to wake to it.

## 2.70
sk: आपूर्यमाणमचलप्रतिष्ठं / समुद्रमापः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् । / तद्वत्कामा यं प्रविशन्ति सर्वे / स शान्तिमाप्नोति न कामकामी ॥
read: aapooryamaanam achala-pratishtham / samudram aapah pravishanti yadvat / tadvat kaamaa yam pravishanti sarve / sa shaantim aapnoti na kaama-kaamee
t: Like the ocean
one: Be like the ocean. Rivers pour into it all day, yet it stays full and still.
meaning: "Rivers flow into the ocean, which is always being filled and yet stays unmoved. In the same way, one into whom all desires flow without disturbing him attains peace, and not the one who runs after desires."
gem: Evenness (wide and unshaken)
s: The wish list that kept growing | Each week someone in your group has something new: a smartwatch, wireless earphones, branded shoes, a gaming chair. You keep a wish list on your phone, and it gets longer every week. When you finally got the earphones, you were happy for about ten days, and then the list grew again. You show the list to your grandfather. He says, "The sea does not run after rivers. It is already full, so the rivers come and go, and it does not change. Be full first." That night you cut the list down to the two things you actually need, and you feel lighter than you did on the day the earphones came.
s: The friend who is not shaken | Something new is pushed at your group almost every day. A classmate says everyone must buy the new game so that you can all play together. Someone's cousin invites the whole group to a party on a school night. An advertisement for the latest shoes follows you from one app to the next. An older boy in the colony dares all of you to try a stunt on your cycles. Most of you jump at each one and feel bad about whatever you miss. One friend listens to all of it, smiles, says yes to the one or two things that suit him and lets the rest go by without any fuss. He never looks as if he is missing out. He looks full. You ask him how he does it. "Wants keep coming, like rivers," he says. "I do not have to run after each one. I already have enough."
s: A festival morning | On a festival morning, everyone wants something from you at once: fetch the flowers, find the lamp, mind your cousin, answer the door. You used to get flustered and snap. Now you take them all in, pick up one at a time and stay level. Your mother asks what has changed.
h: **Let wants and offers arrive without jumping at each one.**
h: **Make your inside bigger,** through sleep, prayer and time with good friends, so that small things do not flood you.
h: **Choose one thing and let the rest flow past.**
try: The next time three things pull at you together, pause, choose one and let the other two go.
bhakti: The ocean is one of the great pictures of God in our tradition. A heart that rests in Him becomes wide like the sea.

## 2.71
sk: विहाय कामान्यः सर्वान्पुमांश्चरति निःस्पृहः । निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः स शान्तिमधिगच्छति ॥
read: vihaaya kaamaan yah sarvaan pumaamsh charati nihsprihah | nirmamo nirahankaarah sa shaantim adhigachchhati
t: Without "me" and "mine"
one: Peace comes to the one who lets go of craving, of "mine" and of "I am the one who did it." It is all right not to have everything for yourself, and what you share has a way of coming back.
meaning: "The person who gives up all cravings and lives free from longing, without the sense of 'mine' and without ego, attains peace."
gem: Same Self in all (sharing, and less "me")
s: The bigger piece | Whenever your mother cuts a mango, you and your sister measure the pieces with your eyes, and one of you always complains. Today you say, "You take the bigger one," and you mean it. She stares at you. The mango tastes just as sweet. The next day she saves you the window seat in the car without being asked. The day after, she shares her new colour pens. You did not ask for any of it. When you stopped guarding your portion, she stopped guarding hers, and both of you ended up with more.
s: Your Saturday | You have kept Saturday for yourself: a late morning, your game and your friends. Then your little brother needs help with his project, and your grandmother wants someone to go with her to the temple. You give up your morning, and you do not mention it to anyone. Two weeks later, you are stuck on the night before your own exhibition, with charts still to cut and paste. Your brother, your grandmother and your father all sit down around the table and work with you until it is finished. Nobody asked them to. The time you gave away came back when you needed it most, and more of it than you gave.
s: Whose choice? | On family outings you always want pizza and your brother always wants dosa, and the evening begins with an argument. Today you say, "Let us go to his place." You expect to feel that you have lost. You do not. The dosa is good, everyone is laughing, and it is a happier evening than any you won by arguing. Next time, before you can open your mouth, your brother says, "Let her choose today."
h: **It is all right not to have everything for yourself.** Your portion, your things, your time, your fun and your choice can all be given up sometimes, and you will still have enough.
h: **Change "mine" to "ours"** wherever you can. Share friends, notes and credit.
h: **Trust that what you share comes back.** It may not come from the same person or on the same day, but it comes, and often as more than you gave. This is the universe's way of returning to us what we shared. So do not give in order to get. Give freely, and watch what happens.
h: **Notice how tight "me" and "mine" feel,** and how light sharing feels.
try: Today, give up one thing that is yours, such as your portion, your turn, your choice or an hour of your time, and do not mention it. Over the next few days, quietly notice what comes back to you.
bhakti: Everything we call "mine" was given by God and belongs to Him. When we share it freely, He sees that we do not run short.

## 2.72
sk: एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ नैनां प्राप्य विमुह्यति । स्थित्वास्यामन्तकालेऽपि ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृच्छति ॥
read: eshaa braahmee sthitih paartha nainaam praapya vimuhyati | sthitvaasyaam anta-kaale 'pi brahma-nirvaanam richchhati
t: The steady state
one: This is the steady state. Once you reach it you are not confused again, and it is never too late to reach it.
meaning: "This, Arjuna, is the state of one who rests in God. Having reached it, a person is not confused again. Staying in it, even at the end of life, one attains oneness with God."
gem: My steady Self
s: The friend who changed | A boy in your class was known for his temper. Over two years, with a patient teacher and a few good friends, he became the calmest person in the room. When someone asks how, he says, "A little every day. I am still learning."
s: Starting late | Your friend used to say, "I am not the spiritual type." In her final year, after a hard time at home, she begins sitting quietly for ten minutes each morning. She tells you, "I wish I had started earlier. But I am glad I have started."
s: Your grandmother's calm | When you visit your grandmother in the holidays, you notice how peaceful she is, whatever the news. You ask her if she was always like this. She laughs. "Not at your age. It came slowly, and it stayed."
h: **Go back over the four Gems** and choose the one you most need now.
h: **Practise a little every day.** The steady state is built in small pieces.
h: **Do not think it is too late or too early.** Begin from where you are.
try: Choose one verse from this chapter to carry with you this month, and read it every morning.
bhakti: The chapter that began with Arjuna's tears ends in peace. Krishna has shown the whole road, and He walks it with us.
