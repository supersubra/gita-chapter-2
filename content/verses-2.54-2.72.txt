# Part 5: The steady mind (2.54–2.72)
intro: Arjuna asks what a steady person is like. Krishna describes someone who is content inside, careful about what the senses take in, and at peace.

## 2.54
who: Arjuna speaking
sk: स्थितप्रज्ञस्य का भाषा समाधिस्थस्य केशव । स्थितधीः किं प्रभाषेत किमासीत व्रजेत किम् ॥
read: sthita-prajnasya kaa bhaashaa samaadhi-sthasya keshava | sthita-dheeh kim prabhaasheta kim aaseeta vrajeta kim
t: What does a steady person look like?
one: Arjuna asks a practical question: how does a steady person speak, sit and move?
meaning: "Krishna, what is a person of steady wisdom like? How does such a person speak, how do they sit, and how do they walk?"
gem: My steady Self (learning from steady people)
next: Krishna will answer in the next eighteen verses, describing a steady person one quality at a time.
s: The calm classmate | One girl in your class never panics before tests and never gossips. You and your friends call her "the monk." One day you ask her seriously, "How do you stay like that?" She is surprised that anyone noticed, and she tells you.
s: The boy everyone goes to | An older boy in your colony is the one everyone goes to in a crisis. You start watching how he behaves: how he listens, how he answers slowly, how he never talks about people behind their backs.
s: Your mother on a bad morning | The tank overflows, the guests arrive early and the gas runs out, all in one hour, and your mother stays level. You ask your father, "Was she always like this?" He says, "Watch what she does each morning. She takes ten quiet minutes before anyone else is awake."
h: **Find one steady person** among your friends, family or teachers.
h: **Watch what they actually do,** in how they speak, wait and react.
h: **Ask them directly.** Most steady people are happy to share how they learned it.
try: Name the steadiest person you know, and write three things they do differently.
bhakti: Arjuna asks to see wisdom in a living person. God often teaches us through the steady people He places around us.

## 2.55
who: Krishna speaking
sk: प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान् । आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ॥
read: prajahaati yadaa kaamaan sarvaan paartha mano-gataan | aatmany evaatmanaa tushtah sthita-prajnas tadochyate
t: Content within
one: A steady person is content inside and does not need a long list of things to feel happy.
meaning: "Arjuna, when a person gives up all the cravings of the mind and is content in the Self alone, that person is said to be of steady wisdom."
gem: Evenness (contentment)
s: The birthday without a party | Your friend's family cannot afford a party this year. She invites three of you home for her mother's lemon rice and a board game. She is so plainly happy that you all agree it is the best birthday of the year.
s: The friend who does not need the plan | Your group's weekend trip is cancelled, and everyone is sulking. One friend shrugs, picks up a football and says, "Ground at five?" His happiness never depended on the trip.
s: The same old watch | Your cousins are always getting new phones, bags and shoes. Your grandfather has had the same watch and the same few shirts for years, and he seems the most at ease of all of you. "I have enough," he says, and he means it.
h: **Notice what you already have** before you look at what is missing.
h: **Enjoy simple time with friends,** with no spending and no plan.
h: **When a want appears, ask,** "Will I be all right without this?" The answer is usually yes.
try: Spend one evening this week with a friend doing something that costs nothing.
bhakti: The one who is content within has found the Lord seated there. No other treasure is needed.

## 2.56
sk: दुःखेष्वनुद्विग्नमनाः सुखेषु विगतस्पृहः । वीतरागभयक्रोधः स्थितधीर्मुनिरुच्यते ॥
read: duhkheshv anudvigna-manaah sukheshu vigata-sprihah | veeta-raaga-bhaya-krodhah sthita-dheer munir uchyate
t: Free of clinging, fear and anger
one: A steady person is not shaken in sorrow, does not chase pleasure, and is free of clinging, fear and anger.
meaning: "One whose mind is not shaken in sorrow, who does not crave pleasure, and who is free from attachment, fear and anger, is called a sage of steady mind."
gem: Evenness
s: The quarrel that did not happen | A friend accuses you, in front of others, of spoiling the group's chart. Your face goes hot. You say, "Let us look at it after the period." By then she has cooled down, you find the real mistake together, and the friendship is unharmed.
s: Fear of being left out | Your group is going to a late-night party you are not comfortable with. You are afraid that if you say no, you will be dropped. You say, "Not this one. Breakfast tomorrow?" They tease you for a minute, and they come to breakfast.
s: The angry message | A friend sends a rude message in the group. You type a sharp reply, then delete it. You call him instead. He has had a terrible day at home and apologises within a minute.
h: **Put time between the feeling and the reply.** Wait an hour, or a night.
h: **Name what you feel.** "I am angry" or "I am afraid" is easier to manage than a storm with no name.
h: **Talk in person** when a message has upset you.
try: The next time you feel angry, count ten slow breaths before you say or type anything.
bhakti: Clinging, fear and anger leave a heart that trusts God. Each time we pause instead of reacting, we make room for that trust.

## 2.57
sk: यः सर्वत्रानभिस्नेहस्तत्तत्प्राप्य शुभाशुभम् । नाभिनन्दति न द्वेष्टि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥
read: yah sarvatraanabhisnehas tat tat praapya shubhaashubham | naabhinandati na dveshti tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa
t: Neither carried away nor bitter
one: A steady person is not carried away by good fortune and does not turn bitter at bad fortune, their own or anyone else's.
meaning: "One who is unattached everywhere, who neither rejoices on meeting good fortune nor hates bad fortune, has steady wisdom."
gem: Evenness (free of comparison and jealousy)
s: Your friend got the prize | Your best friend wins the prize you both wanted. You clap, but inside you feel a pinch. That night you admit it to her. She says, "I felt the same when you won last year." You both laugh, and the pinch goes.
s: The friend with the better phone, cycle and marks | One friend in your group seems to get everything. You notice you have started avoiding him. Then he asks you for help with a family problem, and you see that he too has his share of difficulties.
s: Your sister was chosen first | Your younger sister is chosen for the school team before you ever were. You congratulate her, and you mean most of it. Then you ask her to show you how she practises. She shows you gladly.
h: **Admit jealousy to yourself.** It is a common feeling and it passes sooner when it is seen.
h: **Congratulate the person out loud.** The words help the heart to follow.
h: **Learn from the friend you envy** instead of moving away from them.
try: Congratulate one person today on something they did well, and mean it.
bhakti: The same Lord gives to each of us in His own time. When we are glad for another person's gift, we honour the Giver.

## 2.58
sk: यदा संहरते चायं कूर्मोऽङ्गानीव सर्वशः । इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥
read: yadaa samharate chaayam koormo 'ngaaneeva sarvashah | indriyaaneendriyaarthebhyas tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa
t: Like a tortoise
one: Like a tortoise pulling in its limbs, a steady person can draw the senses back whenever needed.
meaning: "When a person draws the senses back from their objects, as a tortoise draws in its limbs, that person's wisdom is steady."
gem: Mindful action (choosing where my attention goes)
s: Phones in the basket | You and three friends meet to study, and within minutes all four of you are on your phones. One friend puts a basket on the table. "Phones in. We take them out at the break." You get more done in an hour than in the whole of last week.
s: The game at midnight | Your friends play online every night till two, and you join because you do not want to miss out. Your mornings are ruined. You tell them, "I am in till ten, then I am out." They grumble, and then two of them start doing the same.
s: Dinner and the notifications | At dinner, your eyes keep dropping to your phone. Your father turns his face down on the shelf before every meal. You copy him and find that you are actually listening to the conversation.
h: **Decide when to pull in.** Phone away during study, meals and conversations.
h: **Make it easy.** Put the phone in another room, a bag or a basket.
h: **Do it with friends.** A shared rule is easier to keep.
try: Keep your phone out of reach for one full hour today.
bhakti: The tortoise is not afraid of the world. It simply knows when to turn inward. Prayer is our way of turning inward to God.

## 2.59
sk: विषया विनिवर्तन्ते निराहारस्य देहिनः । रसवर्जं रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते ॥
read: vishayaa vinivartante niraahaarasya dehinah | rasa-varjam raso 'py asya param drishtvaa nivartate
t: The taste remains
one: Staying away from something removes the thing but not the taste for it. The taste fades when you find something better.
meaning: "The objects of the senses fall away from one who does not feed on them, but the taste for them remains. Even the taste falls away when one has seen the Highest."
gem: Mindful action (replacing a habit with something better)
s: The deleted app | You delete a game before exams, and for a week you think about it all the time. Then your friend drags you to the ground for football every evening. After a month you realise you have not thought about the game for days.
s: Giving up junk food | You and your brother swear off fried snacks and spend every evening talking about fried snacks. Then you both learn to make a decent sandwich, and the craving becomes much quieter.
s: The scrolling habit | You set a limit on your social apps and keep overriding it. What finally works is joining a friend's evening badminton group. When your evenings are full of something you enjoy, you forget to scroll.
h: **Do not only remove a habit. Replace it** with something you enjoy more.
h: **Expect the craving to stay for a while.** That is normal and not a failure.
h: **Find a friend to share the better thing with.**
try: Choose one habit you want to reduce, and write what you will do instead at that time of day.
bhakti: The higher taste Krishna speaks of is the joy of knowing God. Once we have tasted a little of it, smaller cravings loosen by themselves.

## 2.60
sk: यततो ह्यपि कौन्तेय पुरुषस्य विपश्चितः । इन्द्रियाणि प्रमाथीनि हरन्ति प्रसभं मनः ॥
read: yatato hy api kaunteya purushasya vipashchitah | indriyaani pramaatheeni haranti prasabham manah
t: Even the wise can slip
one: The senses are strong and can carry off even a wise person who is trying hard, so do not be overconfident.
meaning: "Arjuna, the restless senses can forcibly carry away the mind of even a wise person who is striving."
gem: Mindful action (being honest about temptation)
s: "Just one episode" | You are the disciplined one in your group. During study leave you tell yourself, "Just one episode," and look up four hours later. You are shocked at yourself. Your friend says, "It happens to everyone. Give me your password till Friday."
s: The friend who "would never" | Your friend always said he would never copy. In a tough internal exam, an open book is lying within reach, and he glances at it. Afterwards he tells you, ashamed. You do not judge him. You sit apart from the books together next time.
s: The festival sweets | You have decided to cut down on sweets. Then the festival boxes arrive, and by evening you have finished half a box on your own. The next morning you ask your mother to keep the boxes in her cupboard and give you two a day.
h: **Do not say, "I am strong enough."** Keep temptation out of reach instead.
h: **Tell a friend where you are weak,** and let them help.
h: **If you slip, do not hide it.** Admit it, repair it and start again.
try: Put one thing that tempts you out of easy reach, for example by logging out or handing it to a friend.
bhakti: Krishna is frank that this is hard, even for the wise. That is why He asks us, in the next verse, to lean on Him.

## 2.61
sk: तानि सर्वाणि संयम्य युक्त आसीत मत्परः । वशे हि यस्येन्द्रियाणि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥
read: taani sarvaani samyamya yukta aaseeta mat-parah | vashe hi yasyendriyaani tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa
t: Lean on Me
one: Hold the senses steady and fix your mind on God. With His support, self-control becomes possible.
meaning: "Having brought all the senses under control, one should sit steady, with Me as the highest goal. One whose senses are under control has steady wisdom."
gem: My steady Self (support from God)
s: The morning five minutes | Your friend's family sits together for five minutes of prayer before school. You used to think it was old-fashioned. When you stay over one night and join in, you notice how calmly their day begins.
s: The friend who keeps you steady | One friend quietly keeps her fasts and her morning prayer wherever she is. She never preaches. On your worst day, hers is the house you go to, and you ask if you may sit with her while she prays.
s: Before the day begins | You begin each day by checking messages in bed, and you are tense before you get up. A friend suggests two minutes of quiet and a short prayer first. After a week, your mornings feel like your own again.
h: **Give the mind something higher to hold.** Willpower alone gets tired.
h: **Keep a short daily prayer or quiet time,** at the same time every day.
h: **Stay close to friends whose habits steady you.**
try: Before you touch your phone tomorrow morning, sit quietly for two minutes and say a prayer you know.
bhakti: Here Krishna says "Me" for the first time in the chapter. Self-control is not a lonely fight. He is the support.

## 2.62
sk: ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते । सङ्गात्सञ्जायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥
read: dhyaayato vishayaan pumsah sangas teshoopajaayate | sangaat sanjaayate kaamah kaamaat krodho 'bhijaayate
t: How it begins
one: It begins with thinking about something again and again. That grows into attachment, then craving, then anger.
meaning: "When a person keeps thinking about the objects of the senses, attachment to them grows. From attachment comes craving, and from craving comes anger."
gem: Mindful action (catching it early)
s: The shoes | Your friend shows you the shoes he wants. You look at them online every night. Soon you feel you must have them. When your parents say, "Not this month," you slam your door. Later you wonder how a pair of shoes made you shout at your mother.
s: Checking her profile | You keep looking at a classmate's profile and her photos with other friends. First it is curiosity, then you want to be in that group, and then you are angry with her for no reason she would understand.
s: The gift you kept imagining | Your grandfather is bringing a new cycle, and for weeks you imagine yourself riding it. When it turns out to be for your brother, you are furious with him, though he did nothing wrong. You trace the anger back and find that it began with the daydream.
h: **Notice what you keep going back to** in your thoughts and on your screen.
h: **Stop at the first step.** It is far easier to stop looking than to stop being angry.
h: **When you are angry, ask,** "What did I want that I did not get?"
try: Notice one thing you look at again and again, and skip it for one day.
bhakti: What we dwell on shapes us. When the mind dwells on God's name and form, the same ladder leads upward.

## 2.63
sk: क्रोधाद्भवति सम्मोहः सम्मोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः । स्मृतिभ्रंशाद् बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति ॥
read: krodhaad bhavati sammohah sammohaat smriti-vibhramah | smriti-bhramshaad buddhi-naasho buddhi-naashaat pranashyati
t: Where anger leads
one: Anger clouds the mind, the clouded mind forgets what it knows, and then good sense is lost.
meaning: "From anger comes confusion, from confusion loss of memory, and from loss of memory the ruin of understanding. When understanding is ruined, the person is lost."
gem: Evenness (stopping the slide)
s: The words you cannot take back | In a fight, you tell your oldest friend, "I never liked you anyway." It is not true. You forgot, in that moment, ten years of friendship. It takes you three weeks and a long apology to mend what took ten seconds to say.
s: The broken phone | Your friend loses a match online, throws his phone and cracks the screen. He stares at it. "I knew it would break," he says. "I just did not know it in that second."
s: The message sent in anger | Your grandmother tells your parents something you wanted kept quiet, and you send her an angry message. An hour later, you remember all the times she took your side. Your sister helps you find the words, and you go to her room to say sorry in person.
h: **Do nothing important while angry.** Do not speak, send or decide.
h: **Leave the room, drink water, walk.** Understanding returns when the heat goes down.
h: **Repair quickly.** Say sorry the same day if you can.
try: Make a rule with a friend that either of you can say "pause" in a quarrel, and the other will stop.
bhakti: Anger makes us forget who we are and who is in front of us. Saying God's name in that moment helps us remember both.

## 2.64
sk: रागद्वेषवियुक्तैस्तु विषयानिन्द्रियैश्चरन् । आत्मवश्यैर्विधेयात्मा प्रसादमधिगच्छति ॥
read: raaga-dvesha-viyuktais tu vishayaan indriyaish charan | aatma-vashyair vidheyaatmaa prasaadam adhigachchhati
t: Using things without being used
one: You can live among all the things of the world and stay peaceful, if strong likes and dislikes do not rule you.
meaning: "But one who moves among the objects of the senses with self-control, free from attraction and dislike, attains peace."
gem: Evenness (enjoying without being hooked)
s: The friend who can stop | You and your friend both enjoy the same game. He plays for half an hour and stops. You cannot. He is not stricter than you. He says, "I like it. I just like other things too."
s: At the wedding feast | At your cousin's wedding there is every sweet you love. Last time you ate until you felt sick. This time you take what you enjoy, eat slowly with your friends, and stop. You enjoy the whole evening.
s: The match on television | You love watching cricket, and so does your uncle. He sits down for the last ten overs, enjoys every ball and switches off. You learn his method: decide how much you will watch before you sit down.
h: **Enjoy things, and keep the off-switch in your own hand.**
h: **Decide the limit before you start,** not in the middle.
h: **Do not hate the things you are avoiding.** Hating keeps you tied to them as well.
try: Enjoy one treat today, in a fixed amount or for a fixed time, and stop when you said you would.
bhakti: Krishna's word for the peace that follows is prasada, the same word we use for food blessed by God. Peace is His gift to a mind that is not grasping.

## 2.65
sk: प्रसादे सर्वदुःखानां हानिरस्योपजायते । प्रसन्नचेतसो ह्याशु बुद्धिः पर्यवतिष्ठते ॥
read: prasaade sarva-duhkhaanaam haanir asyopajaayate | prasanna-chetaso hy aashu buddhih paryavatishthate
t: What peace does
one: In a peaceful mind, sorrows fade and clear thinking returns quickly.
meaning: "In that peace, all sorrows come to an end. The understanding of a person with a peaceful mind soon becomes steady."
gem: Evenness (peace first, then answers)
s: The problem that solved itself | You and your friend struggle with a maths problem for an hour and grow more and more upset. She says, "Leave it. Let us get some air." On the walk back, the answer comes to you.
s: After the quarrel | You have fought with your brother, and you spend the evening rehearsing arguments. You go for a run. When you return, you can see his side too, and the conversation takes five minutes.
s: The decision after a good night's sleep | At eleven at night, a fight with your friend looks impossible to mend, and you write a long message ending the friendship. Your mother says, "Sleep. Send it tomorrow if you still want to." In the morning it is an ordinary quarrel with an ordinary answer.
h: **When you are stuck, calm down first** and solve the problem afterwards.
h: **Walk, sleep, pray or sit quietly** before a hard conversation.
h: **Do not trust the thoughts of an upset mind** late at night.
try: The next time you are stuck or upset, take a ten-minute walk before you do anything else.
bhakti: Peace is not the prize at the end. It is the ground on which everything else can be done. We can ask God for it first.

## 2.66
sk: नास्ति बुद्धिरयुक्तस्य न चायुक्तस्य भावना । न चाभावयतः शान्तिरशान्तस्य कुतः सुखम् ॥
read: naasti buddhir ayuktasya na chaayuktasya bhaavanaa | na chaabhaavayatah shaantir ashaantasya kutah sukham
t: No peace, no happiness
one: Without a steady mind there is no peace, and without peace there can be no happiness.
meaning: "The unsteady person has no understanding and no power to reflect. Without reflection there is no peace, and without peace, how can there be happiness?"
gem: Evenness
s: The restless holiday | In the holidays you have every freedom: games, shows, food and friends online. Yet by evening you feel dull and irritated. Your friend says the same. You both agree that last year's trek, with no network at all, felt far happier.
s: The party and the walk | At a loud party, you keep checking your phone and comparing yourself with others. The next day you and one friend walk by the lake and talk for an hour. You go home happy and wonder why.
s: The busy, unhappy week | Your week is full of outings, and still you feel empty on Sunday night. A friend who spent her weekend reading and cooking seems far more rested. You ask if you can join her next Saturday.
h: **When you feel dull or irritated, look for what has disturbed your peace.**
h: **Build some quiet into each day,** even ten minutes.
h: **Choose company and activities that leave you calmer,** not more restless.
try: Tonight, write one thing from today that left you peaceful and one that left you restless.
bhakti: Krishna's question, "How can there be happiness without peace?", is worth carrying with us. Peace comes from keeping the mind with Him.

## 2.67
sk: इन्द्रियाणां हि चरतां यन्मनोऽनुविधीयते । तदस्य हरति प्रज्ञां वायुर्नावमिवाम्भसि ॥
read: indriyaanaam hi charataam yan mano 'nuvidheeyate | tad asya harati prajnaam vaayur naavam ivaambhasi
t: A boat in the wind
one: A mind that follows every wandering sense is carried off course, like a boat in the wind.
meaning: "When the mind runs after the wandering senses, it carries away a person's understanding, as the wind carries away a boat on the water."
gem: Mindful action (holding the rudder)
s: One notification | You are writing an essay when your phone buzzes. It is only a meme from a friend. You reply, then see a video, then another. Forty minutes later you cannot remember what the essay was about.
s: "Let's go, everyone is going" | You are saving money for a course. Your friends decide on a sudden trip, then a concert, then new jackets. Each time you go along. At the end of the term, the savings are gone, and so is the course.
s: The noisy house | You sit down to practise, and every sound in the house pulls you in: the television, your brother's game, a phone call in the next room. By evening you have joined in everything and practised nothing. Your sister shuts your door and says, "Decide where you are going before the wind decides."
h: **Know where you are headed this hour** before you begin.
h: **Remove the wind.** Silence notifications and move away from distraction.
h: **When you notice you have drifted, come back without scolding yourself.**
try: Turn off notifications for one app that pulls you away most often.
bhakti: A boat needs a rudder and a steady hand. When we give the rudder of the mind to God, the winds lose their power over us.

## 2.68
sk: तस्माद्यस्य महाबाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः । इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥
read: tasmaad yasya mahaa-baaho nigriheetaani sarvashah | indriyaaneendriyaarthebhyas tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa
t: Master of your senses
one: So the person whose senses are fully in hand is the one whose wisdom is steady.
meaning: "Therefore, mighty Arjuna, one whose senses are completely held back from their objects has steady wisdom."
gem: Mindful action (self-control as strength)
s: The friend who says no easily | When your group pushes someone to try a cigarette behind the school, one friend just says, "No, I'm fine," and changes the subject. Nobody teases him for long. You see that his "no" is a kind of strength, and you stand next to him.
s: The exam month | During exam month, your friend keeps a simple rule: phone with her mother from nine to nine. People call her extreme. After the results, three of them ask her to hold them to the same rule next term.
s: Movies till morning | At a sleepover, your cousins want to watch movies till morning and press you when you refuse. You keep saying, cheerfully, "I am in for one, and then I sleep." In time they stop pressing, and one cousin quietly rolls out her mat next to yours.
h: **Practise saying no in a friendly voice,** without a long explanation.
h: **Set your rule in advance,** so that you are not deciding under pressure.
h: **Stand beside a friend who says no.** It makes it easier for both of you.
try: Say a calm, friendly "no" to one small thing today that you do not really want to do.
bhakti: Self-control is not a cage. It is the strength that lets us offer a whole, undivided mind to God.

## 2.69
sk: या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी । यस्यां जाग्रति भूतानि सा निशा पश्यतो मुनेः ॥
read: yaa nishaa sarva-bhootaanaam tasyaam jaagarti samyamee | yasyaam jaagrati bhootaani saa nishaa pashyato muneh
t: Awake to different things
one: A wise person is awake to what others ignore, and is not excited by what excites everyone else.
meaning: "What is night for all beings is the time of waking for the self-controlled. What all beings are awake to is night for the sage who sees."
gem: My steady Self (my own values)
s: Not interested in the gossip | At lunch, your whole table is excited about who said what about whom. One friend eats quietly and then asks you how your grandmother's operation went. You realise what she pays attention to, and what she does not.
s: Carnatic music and film songs | You have learned Carnatic music since you were small, and you are good at it. Hardly anyone in your class knows what it is. Everyone sings film songs, which have a huge following, and you start hiding your music class as if it were something uncool. Then you sing at a school function. The hall goes quiet, and afterwards the same friends ask, "How do you do that?" You see that what you are good at does not have to be the popular thing. Growing it is what makes you stand out.
s: The early morning | Your cousins stay up late with shows and wake up tired. Your grandfather sleeps at nine and is up at five to walk and pray. They think he is missing out, and he thinks the same about them.
h: **Ask what you value,** not only what everyone around you is excited about.
h: **It is all right to be the only one** who is not interested in something.
h: **Give your best hours** to what matters most to you.
h: **Know what you are good at and keep developing it,** even when it is not the cool thing.
try: Notice one thing "everyone" is excited about that you do not really care for, and let yourself skip it.
bhakti: The devotee is awake to God's presence, which the busy world sleeps through. The quiet of early morning is a good time to wake to it.

## 2.70
sk: आपूर्यमाणमचलप्रतिष्ठं समुद्रमापः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् । तद्वत्कामा यं प्रविशन्ति सर्वे स शान्तिमाप्नोति न कामकामी ॥
read: aapooryamaanam achala-pratishtham samudram aapah pravishanti yadvat | tadvat kaamaa yam pravishanti sarve sa shaantim aapnoti na kaama-kaamee
t: Like the ocean
one: Be like the ocean. Rivers pour into it all day, yet it stays full and still.
meaning: "Rivers flow into the ocean, which is always being filled and yet stays unmoved. In the same way, one into whom all desires flow without disturbing him attains peace, and not the one who runs after desires."
gem: Evenness (wide and unshaken)
s: Everyone's plans | Your phone fills with plans: a movie, a match, a birthday, a sale. Earlier you tried to do everything and ended up tired. Now you read them all, choose one and wish the others well. Your friends notice that you are calmer.
s: The friend who is not shaken | Offers and temptations come to everyone in your group alike. One friend listens, smiles and takes only what suits him. Nothing seems to push him around. You ask him about it. "I do not have to chase everything that comes," he says.
s: A festival morning | On a festival morning, everyone wants something from you at once: fetch the flowers, find the lamp, mind your cousin, answer the door. You used to get flustered and snap. Now you take them all in, pick up one at a time and stay level. Your mother asks what has changed.
h: **Let wants and offers arrive without jumping at each one.**
h: **Make your inside bigger,** through sleep, prayer and time with good friends, so that small things do not flood you.
h: **Choose one thing and let the rest flow past.**
try: The next time three things pull at you together, pause, choose one and let the other two go.
bhakti: The ocean is one of the great pictures of God in our tradition. A heart that rests in Him becomes wide like the sea.

## 2.71
sk: विहाय कामान्यः सर्वान्पुमांश्चरति निःस्पृहः । निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः स शान्तिमधिगच्छति ॥
read: vihaaya kaamaan yah sarvaan pumaamsh charati nihsprihah | nirmamo nirahankaarah sa shaantim adhigachchhati
t: Without "me" and "mine"
one: Peace comes to the one who lets go of craving, of "mine" and of "I am the one who did it."
meaning: "The person who gives up all cravings and lives free from longing, without the sense of 'mine' and without ego, attains peace."
gem: Same Self in all (sharing, and less "me")
s: "My" best friend | Your best friend has started spending time with a new girl, and you feel she is being taken from you. You keep saying "my friend." Then you invite the new girl to sit with both of you. Now there are three of you, and nobody has lost anyone.
s: Who did the most | After the school fest, your team argues about who worked hardest. You know you did a great deal. You say, "We all did it. It went well." The argument ends, and people remember you kindly for it.
s: "My" room | Your cousin comes to stay for a month and shares your room, your desk and your shelf. You begin to say, "That is mine," and stop. The room is fuller and louder and, you have to admit, more fun. You clear half a shelf for him.
h: **Change "mine" to "ours"** wherever you can. Share friends, notes and credit.
h: **Let good work speak.** You do not need to announce your part.
h: **Notice how tight "me" and "mine" feel,** and how light sharing feels.
try: Share something you usually keep for yourself today, such as your notes, your snack or your friend's company.
bhakti: Everything we call "mine" was given by God and belongs to Him. Remembering this turns owning into caring.

## 2.72
sk: एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ नैनां प्राप्य विमुह्यति । स्थित्वास्यामन्तकालेऽपि ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृच्छति ॥
read: eshaa braahmee sthitih paartha nainaam praapya vimuhyati | sthitvaasyaam anta-kaale 'pi brahma-nirvaanam richchhati
t: The steady state
one: This is the steady state. Once you reach it you are not confused again, and it is never too late to reach it.
meaning: "This, Arjuna, is the state of one who rests in God. Having reached it, a person is not confused again. Staying in it, even at the end of life, one attains oneness with God."
gem: My steady Self
s: The friend who changed | A boy in your class was known for his temper. Over two years, with a patient teacher and a few good friends, he became the calmest person in the room. When someone asks how, he says, "A little every day. I am still learning."
s: Starting late | Your friend used to say, "I am not the spiritual type." In her final year, after a hard time at home, she begins sitting quietly for ten minutes each morning. She tells you, "I wish I had started earlier. But I am glad I have started."
s: Your grandmother's calm | When you visit your grandmother in the holidays, you notice how peaceful she is, whatever the news. You ask her if she was always like this. She laughs. "Not at your age. It came slowly, and it stayed."
h: **Go back over the four Gems** and choose the one you most need now.
h: **Practise a little every day.** The steady state is built in small pieces.
h: **Do not think it is too late or too early.** Begin from where you are.
try: Choose one verse from this chapter to carry with you this month, and read it every morning.
bhakti: The chapter that began with Arjuna's tears ends in peace. Krishna has shown the whole road, and He walks it with us.
