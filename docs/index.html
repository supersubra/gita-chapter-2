Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2

A readable edition for young people. All 72 verses.

Theme

Every verse is set out the same way. First comes its message in one line and the Gem it belongs to, then the Sanskrit, how to read it aloud, and the meaning. After that are three real-life moments. Tap a title to open the one that sounds like your day. Each verse ends with what helps, one thing to try today, and a bhakti thought.

The chapter is one conversation, so it is best read in order, a few verses at a time.

Four Gems to carry through the chapter

  • My steady Self: something in me stays steady through every change. This is the root of resilience.
  • Evenness: feelings come and go, and I do not have to be thrown by them.
  • Mindful action: I give my full attention and best effort, and let go of the result.
  • Same Self in all: everyone carries the same dignity. This leaves room for kindness, gratitude, sharing and a big heart, and less room for jealousy.

When it feels heavy. If a feeling stays for many days, talk to a parent, an elder, a close friend, a cousin or a sibling you trust.

Part 1
Arjuna’s sorrow (2.1–2.10)

Arjuna stands between two armies, overwhelmed by love, duty and fear. These ten verses are about what that feels like and how to begin finding your way.

Verse 2.1 · Sanjaya narrating

Krishna lets Arjuna’s tears be seen before He says a word. Caring enough to cry is not weakness.

Gem: Same Self in all (compassion)

तं तथा कृपयाविष्टमश्रुपूर्णाकुलेक्षणम् ।
विषीदन्तमिदं वाक्यमुवाच मधुसूदनः ॥

tam tathaa kripayaa-vishtam ashru-poorna-aakulekshanam | visheedantam idam vaakyam uvaacha madhusoodanah

Arjuna was filled with pity and sorrow, his eyes full of tears and his mind troubled. Then Krishna spoke to him.

Real-life moments

The friend on the list

You are the class leader, and your teacher steps out for a meeting. She says, “Write down the names of anyone who talks.” The room slowly turns noisy, and when she returns, she asks for the list. Your best friend, who shares her lunchbox with you whenever you forget yours, is on it. Your pen stops moving. Your eyes sting, and you cannot tell whether you are more afraid of the teacher or of losing your friend’s trust.

Your brother’s secret

Your younger brother has been skipping his tuition to play cricket, and he has begged you not to tell. He has covered for you more than once. One evening your mother looks straight at you and asks, “Is he going to his class?” You remember your grandfather saying, “Boys don’t cry,” so you hold it in, and the lump in your throat only grows.

The lie your friend asks for

Your closest friend told his father he was at your house all Sunday, and he was not. Now his father is at your door, asking you kindly whether it is true. Your friend has stood by you since you were both six. You look at your feet, and your eyes fill, because you care about him and you cannot say yes.

What helps

  • Let the feeling be real. Tears that come from caring are nothing to be ashamed of.
  • Say it to someone you trust. A parent, an elder, a coach, a close friend or a cousin can help you put it plainly: “I care about this person, and I have to do something hard.”
  • Ask one question: “What is the honest and kind thing to do?”
  • Be firm without being cruel, because the person in front of you carries the same Self as you.

Try this today: Tell one trusted person about something that is weighing on your heart.

Bhakti thought: Krishna does not call Arjuna’s tears wrong. He stays beside him. When love and duty pull in two directions, we can offer both to Him in prayer. The same God sits in the heart of the person we have to disappoint.

Verse 2.2 · Krishna speaking

Fear and confusion are a fog that has come over you. They are not who you are.

Gem: My steady Self

कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम् ।
अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन ॥

kutas tvaa kashmalam idam vishame samupasthitam | anaarya-jushtam asvargyam akeerti-karam arjuna

Krishna said, “Arjuna, how has this fog of confusion come over you at such a serious moment? Good people do not hold on to it. It will not lift you up, and it will not bring you a good name.”

Real-life moments

The night before you sing

You are to sing at the temple festival tomorrow, and you have practised the song for a month. Lying in bed, your mind fills with “What if my voice cracks? What if I forget the words?” By morning you tell your mother, “I don’t want to go.” She does not scold you. She sits beside you and asks softly, “Where is this coming from? I have heard you sing this very song every evening.” Her question gives you a small gap in which to breathe.

At your friend’s gate

You and your friend had a bad quarrel, and you have come to say sorry. At her gate, your hands are cold. “She will shut the door on me. She has already told everyone,” you think, and you turn to leave. Your elder sister, who walked with you, says, “Is that something you know, or is it just the fear talking? Ring the bell.” By the time the door opens, the fog has thinned.

The last penalty

The colony football match has come down to one penalty, and your friends want you to take it. A familiar thought returns: “Everyone here is better than me, and today they will see it.” You ask if someone else could take it. Your friend says calmly, “We chose you because we have watched you score a hundred of these. Is this a fact, or just a feeling?” You place the ball and realise your feet know what to do.

What helps

  • Pause and ask: “Is this a fact, or a fog?”
  • Take three slow breaths.
  • Remember one real proof of your effort, such as the practice you did, the song you learned or the goals you have already scored.
  • Tell someone who knows you, like a parent, a sibling or a close friend. They can often see your steadiness when you cannot.

Try this today: Before something big, say a short prayer: “Lord, be with me. I will do my part.”

Bhakti thought: Krishna’s first words are a question, not a scolding, because He knows Arjuna’s true nature is stronger than the fog. A short prayer before a big moment can bring that steadiness back.

Verse 2.3

Stand up. This heaviness is real, but it is not all of you.

Gem: My steady Self (resilience)

क्लैब्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते ।
क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ठ परन्तप ॥

klaibyam maa sma gamah paartha naitat tvayy upapadyate | kshudram hridaya-daurbalyam tyaktvottishtha parantapa

“Do not give in to this loss of spirit, Arjuna. It does not suit you. Shake off this small weakness of the heart and stand up.”

Real-life moments

The friend who stopped talking

Your closest friend has stopped talking to you after a quarrel. You shut your door and tell your family, “I am never going out to play again.” After a while your father knocks, sits beside you and says gently, “Wash your face. Then walk with me to the shop, and we will think about what you can do.” It is hard to move, but you get up and splash water on your face, and for the first time that day you take a deep breath.

The paper you have to write again

You have failed one subject and will have to write that exam again. You are sure everyone is talking about it, so you stop going to class. “What is the point?” you tell your friend. She does not argue. She says, “Come, let us have tea first. Then we will sit down and plan for the re-exam.” Walking to the tea shop with her is your first step back.

The dish that burned

Relatives are coming for lunch, and you have offered to make the payasam yourself for the first time. It burns, and the smell fills the house. “I will never cook again,” you say, and you walk out of the kitchen. Your mother calls you back. “Everyone in this family has burned it once, including me. Wash the pot. We have time to make it again.” You pick up the pot.

What helps

  • Do one small act of standing up. Wash your face, drink water, step outside or message one person.
  • Make a simple plan for the next small step, with a cousin, a friend or an elder who has been through something similar.
  • Remember: feelings of failure are heavy, but they are not the whole of you.

Try this today: Write down one small thing you will do tomorrow morning.

Bhakti thought: Krishna says “stand up” with great affection, and He believes in Arjuna before Arjuna believes in himself. The word translated as “unmanliness” really means loss of spirit, which can happen to anyone, and anyone can recover it with His grace.

Verse 2.4 · Arjuna speaking

Arjuna asks how he can fight the very teachers he respects most.

Gem: Same Self in all (respect, gratitude and healthy competition)

कथं भीष्ममहं सङ्ख्ये द्रोणं च मधुसूदन ।
इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि पूजार्हावरिसूदन ॥

katham bheeshmam aham sankhye dronam cha madhusoodana | ishubhih pratiyotsyaami poojaarhaav arisoodana

“Krishna, how can I fight Bhishma and Drona with my arrows in battle? They deserve my deepest respect.”

What Krishna will teach next: Your respect for your teachers is good. Krishna will show Arjuna how to keep it and still do his duty.

Real-life moments

Across the board from your father

Your father taught you chess, one patient evening at a time. Today, in the final of the apartment tournament, he is sitting across the board from you. You see a move that will win the game, and your hand stops above the piece. It feels strange to defeat the person who taught you how.

Two sisters, one solo

Your elder sister taught you your first dance steps, and she still corrects your hands before every programme. This year both of you have tried for the same solo, and only one will get it. One small voice inside you is jealous of her, and another feels guilty for feeling that way. You wonder if dancing your very best against her is a kind of betrayal.

Your coach’s mistake

Your coach taught you everything you know about the game, and you owe him a lot. Before the big match he announces the batting order, and you can see a mistake in it: he does not know that one of your teammates is hiding an injury. You feel torn. Correcting him in front of the team seems disrespectful, but staying silent could cost everyone the match.

What helps

  • Say thank you first. Before the moment, silently thank the person who taught you.
  • Do your honest best. Respect does not mean holding back or staying silent.
  • Speak kindly and politely. In the last case, you might say privately, “Sir, may I tell you one thing before we start?”
  • Notice jealousy without feeding it. It is a normal feeling. Remember that the same Self lives in them and in you, and try to be glad for what they have achieved.

Try this today: Send a thank-you message to a teacher or an elder who helped you.

Bhakti thought: The Gita never calls Arjuna’s respect for his elders wrong. A grateful heart is part of devotion, and a good teacher carries a light that comes from God.

Verse 2.5

A win that comes from hurting the people who guided you is not worth having.

Gem: Mindful action (honesty, gratitude and sharing credit)

गुरूनहत्वा हि महानुभावान् श्रेयो भोक्तुं भैक्ष्यमपीह लोके ।
हत्वार्थकामांस्तु गुरूनिहैव भुञ्जीय भोगान् रुधिरप्रदिग्धान् ॥

guroon ahatvaa hi mahaanubhaavaan shreyo bhoktum bhaikshyam apeeha loke | hatvaartha-kaamaams tu guroon ihaiva bhunjeeya bhogaan rudhira-pradigdhaan

“It is better to live on simple food than to harm my noble teachers. If I harmed them, every pleasure I gained would be stained with the pain I caused.”

What Krishna will teach next: Krishna will show Arjuna how to act with a clear conscience.

Real-life moments

The model your brother helped build

You are the younger of two brothers, and all your life you have heard, “Why can’t you be like him?” This year he sat up many nights helping you build your science model. At the exhibition a judge asks, “Did you do all this by yourself?” Your parents are standing right there. For once you could be the brother who is praised. But you remember him holding the torch while you fixed the wires at midnight, and the prize starts to feel empty before you have even won it.

The bigger room

Your elder sister sat with you every evening this year until fractions finally made sense. Now the two of you are fighting over who gets the bigger room, and your father will decide tonight. You know she came home late last week and did not tell him. One sentence from you would win the room. Then you look at your maths notebook, full of her handwriting, and you know you could never enjoy a room won that way.

Two sisters, one song

You learned to sing from your elder sister, and now relatives keep comparing the two of you. At a family function you are to sing together. She has a sore throat today and asks for a song that sits low. You could insist on the one with the high notes, where your voice would shine and hers would break. You think, “Could I look her in the eye tomorrow?” You choose the song she can sing.

What helps

  • Ask yourself: “Will I be able to enjoy this and look myself in the mirror tomorrow?”
  • Choose the smaller success that is honestly yours.
  • Share credit freely. Say out loud who helped you, and name them in front of others.
  • Be big-hearted with your brother or sister. A generous person gives the credit away and still feels rich.

Try this today: Thank, by name, someone who helped you recently.

Bhakti thought: Arjuna chooses gratitude over gain. A heart that stays thankful to its teachers is already close to God, because He lives in them too.

Verse 2.6

Arjuna admits he does not know what is right, and it is honest to say so.

Gem: Evenness (not knowing is okay)

न चैतद्विद्मः कतरन्नो गरीयो यद्वा जयेम यदि वा नो जयेयुः ।
यानेव हत्वा न जिजीविषामस्तेऽवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः ॥

na chaitad vidmah kataran no gareeyo yad vaa jayema yadi vaa no jayeyuh | yaan eva hatvaa na jijeevishaamas te ’vasthitaah pramukhe dhaartaraashtraah

“I do not even know which is better for us: to fight or not to fight, to win or to lose. The very people we would not want to live without are standing in front of us.”

What Krishna will teach next: Krishna will show Arjuna the next step, not the whole road.

Real-life moments

Science, commerce or arts

Everyone is asking whether you will choose science, commerce or arts. Your friends have already decided, and you honestly do not know what you are good at. Your parents have suggested a direction based on what they see in you, and your teachers have their own views. You feel behind, as if everyone else was handed a map. You sit down with your parents and a teacher you trust, and together you look at your interests and strengths one at a time.

Two friends, one side

Two of your closest friends have quarrelled, and each wants you on her side. One says, “If you sit with her, don’t talk to me.” You have heard both stories, and both sound true. You turn it over again and again, and each time you end up exactly where you started, unable to tell who is right.

Cricket or the mridangam

Your cricket coaching and your mridangam class now fall at the same hour, and you must give one up. You love both, and you are fairly good at both. Your mother says she will not choose for you. Neither choice is guaranteed to turn out well, and that is what makes it so hard.

What helps

  • Say it calmly: “I do not know yet, and that is okay.”
  • List what you truly value, and talk to two or three trusted people, such as your parents, an elder in the family or a teacher.
  • Choose the next step, not your whole life.
  • Give it your best, and stay open to changing course.

Try this today: Write down three things you enjoy doing and three things people say you are good at.

Bhakti thought: Arjuna openly says, “I do not know.” That honesty is where he begins to learn. We too can offer our confusion to God and ask, “Show me the next step.”

Verse 2.7

Arjuna stops arguing and asks to be taught. Asking for help is where learning begins.

Gem: Mindful action (learning with an open heart, and respect for elders and teachers)

कार्पण्यदोषोपहतस्वभावः पृच्छामि त्वां धर्मसम्मूढचेताः ।
यच्छ्रेयः स्यान्निश्चितं ब्रूहि तन्मे शिष्यस्तेऽहं शाधि मां त्वां प्रपन्नम् ॥

kaarpanya-doshopahata-svabhaavah prichchhaami tvaam dharma- sammoodha-chetaah | yach chhreyah syaan nishchitam broohi tan me shishyas te ’ham shaadhi maam tvaam prapannam

“My heart is heavy with helplessness, and my mind is confused about what is right. Please tell me clearly what is truly good for me. I am your student, and I have come to you for shelter. Please teach me.”

What Krishna will teach next: Krishna begins His teaching only after Arjuna humbly asks. Everything that follows opens from this one step.

Real-life moments

Too many videos

You want to learn to swim before the summer trip with your cousins. You have watched videos and listened to friends, and each one says something different: kick harder, kick less, hold your breath, never hold your breath. You feel more confused every day. One evening you go to your father. “I do not know how to do this,” you say. “Please teach me.” On Sunday he takes you to the pool and starts with one thing, floating, and you feel your shoulders drop.

“Where do I begin?”

A group of boys has been teasing you for weeks. You have always believed that asking for help is a sign of weakness, so you have kept quiet. Finally you sit down next to your elder brother and say, “I am not sure what to do.” He puts his phone away and says, “Good. Now we can actually start.”

Nodding without understanding

In your sloka class, everyone else seems to know what the verses mean, so you nod along in order not to look foolish. Then the teacher asks you to explain one, and you cannot. After class you go to her and say, “I have not understood this. Can you take me through it?” She says, “I wish I had asked that when I was your age.”

What helps

  • Choose one or two guides, such as a parent, a grandparent, a teacher or a senior, instead of listening to everyone.
  • Say it plainly: “I do not know. Please guide me.”
  • Listen fully. Elders often carry lessons they learned the hard way, so hear them out before you decide.
  • Try what they suggest, and go back to tell them how it went.

Try this today: Write three questions for a person you respect, and ask one of them.

Bhakti thought: Arjuna says, “I am your student, teach me.” Asking for guidance with an open heart is itself a prayer, and the wisest teacher we meet carries a light from God.

Verse 2.8

No prize, however big, can fill an ache on the inside.

Gem: Evenness (what truly fills the heart: gratitude and contentment)

न हि प्रपश्यामि ममापनुद्याद् यच्छोकमुच्छोषणमिन्द्रियाणाम् ।
अवाप्य भूमावसपत्नमृद्धं राज्यं सुराणामपि चाधिपत्यम् ॥

na hi prapashyaami mamaapanudyaad yach chhokam uchchhoshanam indriyaanaam | avaapya bhoomaav asapatnam riddham raajyam suraanaam api chaadhipatyam

“I cannot see anything that will take away this sorrow that is drying up my strength, even if I won a rich kingdom on earth with no rivals, or even became ruler over the gods.”

What Krishna will teach next: Krishna will now turn Arjuna’s eyes inward.

Real-life moments

Top marks, empty night

You score top marks, and everyone congratulates you. Your family is proud, and your phone is full of messages. That night, you lie awake feeling strangely empty. You think, “I have everything I wanted, so why do I feel like this?” You say nothing, because everyone has told you that you should be happy.

The phone you begged for

After a year of asking, you get the phone you wanted. For a week you are thrilled. Then you feel flat. You scroll for hours without enjoying anything, and you notice yourself comparing your phone with a friend’s newer one. You tell yourself you have no right to complain, when your parents stretched to buy it.

In the group at last

For a whole year you wanted to be part of one particular group, and now they have made room for you. You sit with them and laugh at their jokes. On Sunday evening the same quiet heaviness returns. You find yourself missing the old friend you stopped sitting with, and already worrying about how to keep your place.

What helps

  • Say it out loud to a parent, a sibling, a friend or an elder you trust: “I got what I wanted, and I still feel empty.”
  • Let thankfulness and emptiness sit together. Both can be true.
  • Count what you already have. A gratitude list slowly changes what you notice.
  • Feed the inside. Spend time on a walk, a prayer, music, or helping someone.

Try this today: Write three things you are grateful for, and tell one person about them.

If the heaviness stays for many days, talk to a family member you trust, such as a parent, a grandparent or an elder.

Bhakti thought: Even a great warrior like Arjuna learned that no kingdom can fill the heart. The lasting fullness comes from within, from the Self and from God.

Verse 2.9 · Sanjaya narrating

Arjuna runs out of words and goes silent. Silence is not the end of the story.

Gem: My steady Self (silence is not the end)

एवमुक्त्वा हृषीकेशं गुडाकेशः परन्तप ।

What Krishna will teach next: Krishna is still right there in the chariot. He does not leave when Arjuna goes silent.

Real-life moments

“I am done with everything”

After a lost match, an argument with a close friend and a scolding at home, you say to yourself, “I am done with everything.” You stop answering messages and lie quietly in the dark. You have not decided anything. You just have no words left.

Dinner going cold

You and your sister have had a fight in which both of you said too much. You lock your door and lie on the bed. Your mother calls you for dinner three times, and you do not answer, because you do not know how to explain.

Muting the group

Your friends made a plan without you, and you found out from their photos. You mute the group, leave your phone inside and sit on the terrace, unable to reply to anyone. It feels as if it makes no difference whether you are there or not.

What helps

  • Go easy on yourself.
  • Do one tiny thing: drink water, eat something, step outside.
  • Send one line to a friend, a sibling or a parent: “I am not okay right now.”
  • If the silence lasts for days, tell a parent, a grandparent or an elder you trust.

Try this today: Send that one line to one person.

Bhakti thought: Arjuna’s silence is not the end of the story, because Krishna waits right beside him. When you have no words left, a silent prayer is enough.

Krishna smiles in the middle of the battlefield. His calm says the storm is not as big as it feels.

Then, in the middle of the two armies, Krishna spoke these words to the sorrowful Arjuna, with a gentle smile.

Real-life moments

Grandmother’s warm milk

You come home upset after a quarrel with a friend and sit quietly in your room. Your grandmother does not ask a hundred questions or give a lecture. She sits beside you, hands you a glass of warm milk and says, “Drink this first. Then we will talk.” Her calm makes the problem feel a little smaller.

The cousin who listens

You were left out of the team, and you feel low. Your elder cousin, who went through the same thing a few years ago, calls in the evening. He does not tell you what to do. He says, “Tell me everything, I am listening.” By the end of the call, you are laughing at something small.

Sitting with your little brother

Your younger brother comes home, throws his bag down and will not say what happened. You do not rush in with advice or questions. You sit beside him, peel an orange, pass him half and say, “Whenever you want to talk, I am here.” Your calm gives him room to breathe.

What helps

  • Let a calm person in, such as a grandparent, an elder you trust or a close friend.
  • When someone else is struggling, sit beside them and listen first. Sharing your time and calm is a gift.
  • Take three slow breaths before you speak to anyone who is upset.

Try this today: Offer to sit with someone who looks like they are having a hard day.

Bhakti thought: Krishna stays steady in the middle of Arjuna’s storm. The same God lives in the heart of the person who is hurting, so when we sit calmly with them, we are keeping Him company too.

Verse 2.11 · Krishna speaking

You already have the wisdom you give to others. Use it on yourself.

Real-life moments

The friend everyone asks

You are the friend everyone calls for advice. When a classmate is teased about her voice, you tell her, “Their words don’t decide who you are,” and you mean it. Then someone laughs at your drawing, and you sulk for days, forgetting every word you said. Your elder sibling notices and asks, “What would you tell your friend if she were in your place?”

When a friend drifts away

You comforted a friend when her best friend left her for another group. “You are worth more than this, and time will help,” you said. A few months later, your own close friend drifts away, and you cannot hear those words at all. A cousin who knew about the earlier conversation gently reminds you, “You said the same thing to your friend, remember?”

“Mistakes are how we learn”

When your little sister spills paint on her project, you tell her, “Mistakes are how we learn.” Then you miss an easy goal in front of everyone and scold yourself for a week. She listens to you muttering and asks quietly, “Would you speak to me the way you are speaking to yourself?”

What helps

  • Hear your own advice as if a friend were saying it to you.
  • Write down the kind words you give to others, and read them on a hard day.
  • Ask an elder sibling, a cousin or a close friend to remind you of them when you forget.

Try this today: Write one piece of advice you once gave someone, and read it to yourself.

Bhakti thought: Krishna begins with honesty and affection. He sees the wise person in Arjuna before Arjuna does. The wisdom you give to others comes from the same Self within you.

Verse 2.12

You have always been you, and you always will be. No group, title or result can change that.

न चैव न भविष्यामः सर्वे वयमतः परम् ॥

Real-life moments

Left out of the group

The group of friends you shared your lunch with starts leaving you out. It feels as if you have disappeared. At home, one of your parents sits with you and says gently, “You were you before this group, and you will still be you after it.”

Not selected

You trained for months for a place on the team, and you did not get selected. For a long time you thought of yourself as “the sports person,” and now you feel like nobody. A grandparent or an elder sibling says, “Who you are is far bigger than a title or a team.”

A new town

Your family moves to a new town. In the old one, everybody knew you as the singer, the one who made the whole class laugh. Here nobody knows your name, and you wonder who you are without all that. In the evening your father says, “Your talent, your values and your kindness came with us on the same train. They are still yours.”

What helps

  • Write a short list of who you are beyond one group, one result or one title. Include your values, your strengths and the people who love you.
  • Ask a parent, an elder or a friend to add to it.
  • Remind yourself each morning: “I am more than today’s result.”

Try this today: Add one thing to your list that no result or title can take away.

Bhakti thought: Krishna says “I, you and all of these,” which means every one of us has always been held in His presence. We are never truly alone, even when a group, a title or a place disappears.

Verse 2.13

Life moves from one stage to the next, and the same “you” goes through all of them.

Gem: My steady Self (change is natural, and I stay)

देहिनोऽस्मिन्यथा देहे कौमारं यौवनं जरा ।
तथा देहान्तरप्राप्तिर्धीरस्तत्र न मुह्यति ॥

dehino ’smin yathaa dehe kaumaaram yauvanam jaraa | tathaa dehaantara- praaptir dheeras tatra na muhyati

“Just as the one who lives in this body passes through childhood, youth and old age, in the same way it passes on to another body. A steady person is not troubled by this.”

Real-life moments

Different sections

You have just moved up to a new class, and your close friends have been placed in different sections. You scroll through old photos one night and feel a pang. Your elder sibling sits down beside you and says, “Look how much you have grown since those photos, and you are still you. This is just a new chapter.”

A body that keeps changing

You have grown so fast this year that nothing fits, and your voice cracks in the middle of a sentence. Your friends laugh, and you feel like a stranger in your own body. Your father shows you a photograph of himself at your age, all elbows and knees. “The one who lives inside is the same as last year,” he says. “Only the outside is changing.”

Your sister’s empty room

Your elder sister has left home for college, and her room is very quiet. You even miss fighting with her over the mirror every morning. Your grandmother says on the phone, “This is another stage for the whole family, like going from childhood to youth. Keep what was good, and welcome what is new.”

What helps

  • See change as a stage, not a loss. Think of how much you have already changed since childhood, and notice that you are still you.
  • Keep one thing from each stage, such as a friend, a habit or a photo.
  • Welcome one new thing this week, like a new class, a new place or a new person.

Try this today: Send a message to one friend or cousin from an earlier chapter of your life.

Bhakti thought: The same “I” has been with you through every stage, and God has been with that “I” all along. He holds the child, the young person and the elder equally.

Verse 2.14

Pleasant and unpleasant feelings both come and go. Bear them patiently, and they pass.

Gem: Evenness and resilience

मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः ।
आगमापायिनोऽनित्यास्तांस्तितिक्षस्व भारत ॥

maatraa-sparshaas tu kaunteya sheetoshna-sukha-duhkhadaah | aagamaapaayino ’nityaas taams titikshasva bhaarata

“The touch of the world on our senses brings cold and heat, pleasure and pain. They come and they go, and none of them lasts. So bear them patiently, Arjuna.”

Real-life moments

The mocking comment

You post a video of a song you have practised for weeks, and a stranger leaves a mocking comment. It stings all evening, and you keep checking your phone to see if anyone else has said anything. A few days later, your cousin brings it up, and you have to think hard to remember what the comment said.

Chores on a hot afternoon

It is a hot afternoon, and your mother asks you to help clean the house before guests arrive. You would much rather lie down with your phone. The work is tiring and dull, and your first thought is to find a way out of it. But after a few minutes of starting, the discomfort fades, and by evening you feel proud that the house looks bright and your mother looks relaxed.

The scolding

Your father scolds you sharply at nine at night for coming home late. Your stomach tightens, and you think about it all through dinner and for a long time in bed. The next morning he asks, “Shall I drop you at the bus stop?” as if nothing had happened, and you realise the sting lasted much longer in your mind than in his.

What helps

  • Say, “This will pass.” Heat and cold, pleasure and pain all come and go.
  • Take three slow breaths before you reply to anything that stings.
  • Do the next small task even while you feel uncomfortable. Many unpleasant things fade once you begin.
  • Talk it over with a friend, a sibling or a parent if it still hurts after a day or two.

Try this today: Do one small task you have been avoiding, such as a chore at home, for just ten minutes.

Bhakti thought: Krishna calls Arjuna “Bharata”, a member of a great family, as if to say, “You already have the strength to bear this.” When we offer our discomfort to God, it feels lighter.

Verse 2.15

Staying the same person on good days and bad days is the way to lasting peace.

Gem: Evenness

यं हि न व्यथयन्त्येते पुरुषं पुरुषर्षभ ।
समदुःखसुखं धीरं सोऽमृतत्वाय कल्पते ॥

yam hi na vyathayanty ete purusham purusharshabha | sama-duhkha- sukham dheeram so ’mritatvaaya kalpate

“The steady person, who is not shaken by these feelings and stays the same in pain and in pleasure, becomes ready for lasting freedom.”

Real-life moments

The full house and the quiet house

Your cousins come to stay for the festival, and for four days the house is full of noise, games and sweets. You float through every one of them. Then they leave, and you sulk for three days and snap at everyone. Your mother says gently, “The house was full, and now it is quiet. You are the same child in both.”

The cheer and the dropped catch

You play brilliantly in the first match of the tournament and hear the cheers. In the second match, you drop an easy catch, and your mood sinks so low that you cannot eat dinner. An older player who has been at it for years tells you, “The best players stay steady after both a cheer and a mistake.”

Carrom with your brother

You and your brother play carrom every evening. When you win, you dance around the room and tease him. When you lose, you push the board away and say he cheated. One evening he says, “I don’t mind losing to you. I mind playing with you.” It strikes you that winning and losing are both turning you into someone you do not like.

What helps

  • Enjoy the good moments without boasting, and face the hard ones without sinking.
  • Pause before you share good news online or react to bad news. Ask, “Will this matter next month?”
  • Learn from a steady person. Watch how a grandparent, a teacher or a senior behaves after both good days and bad days.

Try this today: The next time something good or bad happens, take one slow breath before you react.

Bhakti thought: Evenness is a gift that grows with practice and with grace. A heart that rests in God is like a calm lake, which reflects both sunshine and storm without losing its depth.

Verse 2.16

Much of what feels so important today is only passing. Look for what truly lasts.

उभयोरपि दृष्टोऽन्तस्त्वनयोस्तत्त्वदर्शिभिः ॥

“What is not lasting has no true existence, and what is truly real never ceases to be. Those who see the truth have understood the nature of both.”

Real-life moments

A classmate’s photos

You scroll through a classmate’s holiday photos, new phone and party pictures, and feel that your own life is dull. But a picture shows only one moment. It does not show the long drive, the quarrel in the car or the homework waiting at home. What stays from any holiday is how people treated one another.

Brand pressure

Your friends begin comparing brands: shoes, phones, watches. You feel the pressure to buy the latest thing, even though your family has to stretch to afford it. In a year those shoes will be worn out, and a newer phone will be in every shop. The evening you all got drenched cycling home in the rain will still make you laugh ten years from now.

A friend’s highlights

A friend posts about a prize, a new bicycle and a trip, all in one month. You feel a sting of jealousy and wonder if you are falling behind. A post is a highlight, not a whole life. The same friend has dull days and bad days that are never posted. Your own steady habits, which nobody photographs, will take you further than one good month.

What helps

  • Ask, “Is this passing or lasting?” A trend, a look or a number of likes passes. Honesty, learning and kindness last.
  • Spend your time and money on what lasts: your learning, your health and your friendships.
  • When jealousy comes, name it, and then wish the other person well. It loosens its grip.

Try this today: Write down one thing you enjoy that no one can see online, and be thankful for it.

Bhakti thought: Krishna teaches us to look for what truly lasts, and that search leads us to Him.

Verse 2.19

Called useless

In an argument, a friend calls you useless, and you carry that word for days as if it were the truth about you. Then you remember that last month the same friend called you the best friend he had. Neither word changed what you are.

  • Ask an elder or a friend you trust to tell you honestly how your words sound to others.

समाधावचला बुद्धिस्तदा योगमवाप्स्यसि ॥

shruti-vipratipannaa te yadaa sthaasyati nishchalaa | samaadhaav achalaa buddhis tadaa yogam avaapsyasi

“When your mind, now confused by all you have heard, stands firm and steady within, then you will reach yoga.”

Real-life moments

Two friends, two opinions

One friend says, “Take biology.” Another says, “Take computers.” Each time you talk to one of them, you change your mind. Finally you sit alone for an evening with a notebook. In the quiet, you already know which one you want.

The noisy feed

Before every decision, you ask in three groups and watch five videos. You end up more confused. A friend suggests one day with no feed. By evening your own thoughts are clear enough to hear.

After all the advice

You have a job choice to make, and you have heard from your parents, friends and mentors. You thank them all. Then you take a long walk by yourself. When you return, you know your answer, and you feel calm about it.

What helps

  • Listen to advice, and then stop collecting it.
  • Give yourself quiet time with no phone before a decision.
  • Trust the answer that comes when you are calm, not the one that comes when you are rushed.

Try this today: Sit quietly for five minutes today with no phone, no music and no book.

Bhakti thought: When the mind is still, it rests in God. That stillness is what Krishna calls samadhi.

Part 5
The steady mind (2.54–2.72)

Arjuna asks what a steady person is like. Krishna describes someone who is content inside, careful about what the senses take in, and at peace.

Verse 2.54 · Arjuna speaking

Arjuna asks a practical question: how does a steady person speak, sit and move?

Gem: My steady Self (learning from steady people)

स्थितप्रज्ञस्य का भाषा समाधिस्थस्य केशव ।
स्थितधीः किं प्रभाषेत किमासीत व्रजेत किम् ॥

sthita-prajnasya kaa bhaashaa samaadhi-sthasya keshava | sthita-dheeh kim prabhaasheta kim aaseeta vrajeta kim

“Krishna, what is a person of steady wisdom like? How does such a person speak, how do they sit, and how do they walk?”

What Krishna will teach next: Krishna will answer in the next eighteen verses, describing a steady person one quality at a time.

Real-life moments

The calm classmate

One girl in your class never panics before tests and never gossips. You and your friends call her “the monk.” One day you ask her seriously, “How do you stay like that?” She is surprised that anyone noticed, and she tells you.

The senior everyone respects

A senior in your hostel is the one everyone goes to in a crisis. You start watching how he behaves: how he listens, how he answers slowly, how he never talks about people behind their backs.

Your first good manager

Your manager stays level when a client shouts. You ask a friend on the team, “Was she always like this?” He says, “Watch what she does before each call. She takes two minutes alone.”

What helps

  • Find one steady person among your friends, family or teachers.
  • Watch what they actually do, in how they speak, wait and react.
  • Ask them directly. Most steady people are happy to share how they learned it.

Try this today: Name the steadiest person you know, and write three things they do differently.

Bhakti thought: Arjuna asks to see wisdom in a living person. God often teaches us through the steady people He places around us.

Verse 2.55 · Krishna speaking

A steady person is content inside and does not need a long list of things to feel happy.

Gem: Evenness (contentment)

प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान् ।
आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ॥

prajahaati yadaa kaamaan sarvaan paartha mano-gataan | aatmany evaatmanaa tushtah sthita-prajnas tadochyate

“Arjuna, when a person gives up all the cravings of the mind and is content in the Self alone, that person is said to be of steady wisdom.”

Real-life moments

The birthday without a party

Your friend’s family cannot afford a party this year. She invites three of you home for her mother’s lemon rice and a board game. She is so plainly happy that you all agree it is the best birthday of the year.

The friend who does not need the plan

Your group’s weekend trip is cancelled, and everyone is sulking. One friend shrugs, picks up a football and says, “Ground at five?” His happiness never depended on the trip.

The simple desk

Your colleagues are always upgrading their phones, bags and watches. One friend has had the same things for years and seems the most at ease of all of you. “I have enough,” he says, and he means it.

What helps

  • Notice what you already have before you look at what is missing.
  • Enjoy simple time with friends, with no spending and no plan.
  • When a want appears, ask, “Will I be all right without this?” The answer is usually yes.

Try this today: Spend one evening this week with a friend doing something that costs nothing.

Bhakti thought: The one who is content within has found the Lord seated there. No other treasure is needed.

Verse 2.56

A steady person is not shaken in sorrow, does not chase pleasure, and is free of clinging, fear and anger.

Gem: Evenness

दुःखेष्वनुद्विग्नमनाः सुखेषु विगतस्पृहः ।
वीतरागभयक्रोधः स्थितधीर्मुनिरुच्यते ॥

duhkheshv anudvigna-manaah sukheshu vigata-sprihah | veeta-raaga-bhaya-krodhah sthita-dheer munir uchyate

“One whose mind is not shaken in sorrow, who does not crave pleasure, and who is free from attachment, fear and anger, is called a sage of steady mind.”

Real-life moments

The quarrel that did not happen

A friend accuses you, in front of others, of spoiling the group’s chart. Your face goes hot. You say, “Let us look at it after the period.” By then she has cooled down, you find the real mistake together, and the friendship is unharmed.

Fear of being left out

Your group is going to a late-night party you are not comfortable with. You are afraid that if you say no, you will be dropped. You say, “Not this one. Breakfast tomorrow?” They tease you for a minute, and they come to breakfast.

The angry message

A colleague sends a rude message in the team group. You type a sharp reply, then delete it. You call him instead. He has had a terrible day and apologises within a minute.

What helps

  • Put time between the feeling and the reply. Wait an hour, or a night.
  • Name what you feel. “I am angry” or “I am afraid” is easier to manage than a storm with no name.
  • Talk in person when a message has upset you.

Try this today: The next time you feel angry, count ten slow breaths before you say or type anything.

Bhakti thought: Clinging, fear and anger leave a heart that trusts God. Each time we pause instead of reacting, we make room for that trust.

Verse 2.57

A steady person is not carried away by good fortune and does not turn bitter at bad fortune, their own or anyone else’s.

Gem: Evenness (free of comparison and jealousy)

यः सर्वत्रानभिस्नेहस्तत्तत्प्राप्य शुभाशुभम् ।
नाभिनन्दति न द्वेष्टि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥

yah sarvatraanabhisnehas tat tat praapya shubhaashubham | naabhinandati na dveshti tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa

“One who is unattached everywhere, who neither rejoices on meeting good fortune nor hates bad fortune, has steady wisdom.”

Real-life moments

Your friend got the prize

Your best friend wins the prize you both wanted. You clap, but inside you feel a pinch. That night you admit it to her. She says, “I felt the same when you won last year.” You both laugh, and the pinch goes.

The friend with the better phone, bike and marks

One friend in your hostel seems to get everything. You notice you have started avoiding him. Then he asks you for help with a family problem, and you see that he too has his share of difficulties.

The colleague who was promoted first

A friend who joined with you is promoted before you. You congratulate her, and you mean most of it. You decide to take her out for coffee and ask what she did well. She tells you gladly.

What helps

  • Admit jealousy to yourself. It is a common feeling and it passes sooner when it is seen.
  • Congratulate the person out loud. The words help the heart to follow.
  • Learn from the friend you envy instead of moving away from them.

Try this today: Congratulate one person today on something they did well, and mean it.

Bhakti thought: The same Lord gives to each of us in His own time. When we are glad for another person’s gift, we honour the Giver.

Verse 2.58

Like a tortoise pulling in its limbs, a steady person can draw the senses back whenever needed.

Gem: Mindful action (choosing where my attention goes)

यदा संहरते चायं कूर्मोऽङ्गानीव सर्वशः ।
इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥

yadaa samharate chaayam koormo ’ngaaneeva sarvashah | indriyaaneendriyaarthebhyas tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa

“When a person draws the senses back from their objects, as a tortoise draws in its limbs, that person’s wisdom is steady.”

Real-life moments

Phones in the basket

You and three friends meet to study, and within minutes all four of you are on your phones. One friend puts a basket on the table. “Phones in. We take them out at the break.” You get more done in an hour than in the whole of last week.

The game at midnight

Your hostel friends play online every night till two, and you join because you do not want to miss out. Your mornings are ruined. You tell them, “I am in till eleven, then I am out.” They grumble, and then two of them start doing the same.

The meeting and the notifications

In meetings, your eyes keep dropping to your phone. A friend at work turns hers face down at the start of every meeting. You copy her and find that you are actually listening.

What helps

  • Decide when to pull in. Phone away during study, meals and conversations.
  • Make it easy. Put the phone in another room, a bag or a basket.
  • Do it with friends. A shared rule is easier to keep.

Try this today: Keep your phone out of reach for one full hour today.

Bhakti thought: The tortoise is not afraid of the world. It simply knows when to turn inward. Prayer is our way of turning inward to God.

Verse 2.59

Staying away from something removes the thing but not the taste for it. The taste fades when you find something better.

Gem: Mindful action (replacing a habit with something better)

विषया विनिवर्तन्ते निराहारस्य देहिनः ।
रसवर्जं रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते ॥

vishayaa vinivartante niraahaarasya dehinah | rasa-varjam raso ’py asya param drishtvaa nivartate

“The objects of the senses fall away from one who does not feed on them, but the taste for them remains. Even the taste falls away when one has seen the Highest.”

Real-life moments

The deleted app

You delete a game before exams, and for a week you think about it all the time. Then your friend drags you to the ground for football every evening. After a month you realise you have not thought about the game for days.

Giving up junk food

You and your roommate swear off fried snacks and spend every evening talking about fried snacks. Then you both learn to make a decent sandwich, and the craving becomes much quieter.

The scrolling habit

You set a limit on your social apps and keep overriding it. What finally works is joining a friend’s evening badminton group. When your evenings are full of something you enjoy, you forget to scroll.

What helps

  • Do not only remove a habit. Replace it with something you enjoy more.
  • Expect the craving to stay for a while. That is normal and not a failure.
  • Find a friend to share the better thing with.

Try this today: Choose one habit you want to reduce, and write what you will do instead at that time of day.

Bhakti thought: The higher taste Krishna speaks of is the joy of knowing God. Once we have tasted a little of it, smaller cravings loosen by themselves.

Verse 2.60

The senses are strong and can carry off even a wise person who is trying hard, so do not be overconfident.

Gem: Mindful action (being honest about temptation)

यततो ह्यपि कौन्तेय पुरुषस्य विपश्चितः ।
इन्द्रियाणि प्रमाथीनि हरन्ति प्रसभं मनः ॥

yatato hy api kaunteya purushasya vipashchitah | indriyaani pramaatheeni haranti prasabham manah

“Arjuna, the restless senses can forcibly carry away the mind of even a wise person who is striving.”

Real-life moments

“Just one episode”

You are the disciplined one in your group. During study leave you tell yourself, “Just one episode,” and look up four hours later. You are shocked at yourself. Your friend says, “It happens to everyone. Give me your password till Friday.”

The friend who “would never”

Your friend always said he would never copy. In a tough internal exam, an open book is lying within reach, and he glances at it. Afterwards he tells you, ashamed. You do not judge him. You sit apart from the books together next time.

The sale

You are careful with money. Then a midnight sale begins, and by one o’clock you have ordered things you do not need. In the morning you cancel half of them and remove your saved card.

What helps

  • Do not say, “I am strong enough.” Keep temptation out of reach instead.
  • Tell a friend where you are weak, and let them help.
  • If you slip, do not hide it. Admit it, repair it and start again.

Try this today: Put one thing that tempts you out of easy reach, for example by logging out or handing it to a friend.

Bhakti thought: Krishna is frank that this is hard, even for the wise. That is why He asks us, in the next verse, to lean on Him.

Verse 2.61

Hold the senses steady and fix your mind on God. With His support, self-control becomes possible.

Gem: My steady Self (support from God)

तानि सर्वाणि संयम्य युक्त आसीत मत्परः ।
वशे हि यस्येन्द्रियाणि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥

taani sarvaani samyamya yukta aaseeta mat-parah | vashe hi yasyendriyaani tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa

“Having brought all the senses under control, one should sit steady, with Me as the highest goal. One whose senses are under control has steady wisdom.”

Real-life moments

The morning five minutes

Your friend’s family sits together for five minutes of prayer before school. You used to think it was old-fashioned. When you stay over one night and join in, you notice how calmly their day begins.

The friend who keeps you steady

In college, one friend quietly keeps her fasts and her morning prayer even in the hostel. She never preaches. On your worst day, hers is the room you go to, and you ask if you may sit with her while she prays.

Before the workday

You begin each day by checking messages in bed, and you are tense before you get up. A friend suggests two minutes of quiet and a short prayer first. After a week, your mornings feel like your own again.

What helps

  • Give the mind something higher to hold. Willpower alone gets tired.
  • Keep a short daily prayer or quiet time, at the same time every day.
  • Stay close to friends whose habits steady you.

Try this today: Before you touch your phone tomorrow morning, sit quietly for two minutes and say a prayer you know.

Bhakti thought: Here Krishna says “Me” for the first time in the chapter. Self-control is not a lonely fight. He is the support.

Verse 2.62

It begins with thinking about something again and again. That grows into attachment, then craving, then anger.

Gem: Mindful action (catching it early)

ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते ।
सङ्गात्सञ्जायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥

dhyaayato vishayaan pumsah sangas teshoopajaayate | sangaat sanjaayate kaamah kaamaat krodho ’bhijaayate

“When a person keeps thinking about the objects of the senses, attachment to them grows. From attachment comes craving, and from craving comes anger.”

Real-life moments

The shoes

Your friend shows you the shoes he wants. You look at them online every night. Soon you feel you must have them. When your parents say, “Not this month,” you slam your door. Later you wonder how a pair of shoes made you shout at your mother.

Checking her profile

You keep looking at a classmate’s profile and her photos with other friends. First it is curiosity, then you want to be in that group, and then you are angry with her for no reason she would understand.

The role you kept imagining

You daydream every day about leading the new project. When it goes to a friend, you are furious with him, though he did nothing wrong. You trace the anger back and find that it began with the daydream.

What helps

  • Notice what you keep going back to in your thoughts and on your screen.
  • Stop at the first step. It is far easier to stop looking than to stop being angry.
  • When you are angry, ask, “What did I want that I did not get?”

Try this today: Notice one thing you look at again and again, and skip it for one day.

Bhakti thought: What we dwell on shapes us. When the mind dwells on God’s name and form, the same ladder leads upward.

Verse 2.63

Anger clouds the mind, the clouded mind forgets what it knows, and then good sense is lost.

Gem: Evenness (stopping the slide)

क्रोधाद्भवति सम्मोहः सम्मोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः ।
स्मृतिभ्रंशाद् बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति ॥

krodhaad bhavati sammohah sammohaat smriti-vibhramah | smriti-bhramshaad buddhi-naasho buddhi-naashaat pranashyati

“From anger comes confusion, from confusion loss of memory, and from loss of memory the ruin of understanding. When understanding is ruined, the person is lost.”

Real-life moments

The words you cannot take back

In a fight, you tell your oldest friend, “I never liked you anyway.” It is not true. You forgot, in that moment, ten years of friendship. It takes you three weeks and a long apology to mend what took ten seconds to say.

The broken phone

Your friend loses a match online, throws his phone and cracks the screen. He stares at it. “I knew it would break,” he says. “I just did not know it in that second.”

The email sent in anger

You reply to a senior in anger and copy the whole team. An hour later, you remember all the times she supported you. A friend helps you write the apology, and you walk over to say it in person.

What helps

  • Do nothing important while angry. Do not speak, send or decide.
  • Leave the room, drink water, walk. Understanding returns when the heat goes down.
  • Repair quickly. Say sorry the same day if you can.

Try this today: Make a rule with a friend that either of you can say “pause” in a quarrel, and the other will stop.

Bhakti thought: Anger makes us forget who we are and who is in front of us. Saying God’s name in that moment helps us remember both.

Verse 2.64

You can live among all the things of the world and stay peaceful, if strong likes and dislikes do not rule you.

Gem: Evenness (enjoying without being hooked)

रागद्वेषवियुक्तैस्तु विषयानिन्द्रियैश्चरन् ।
आत्मवश्यैर्विधेयात्मा प्रसादमधिगच्छति ॥

raaga-dvesha-viyuktais tu vishayaan indriyaish charan | aatma-vashyair vidheyaatmaa prasaadam adhigachchhati

“But one who moves among the objects of the senses with self-control, free from attraction and dislike, attains peace.”

Real-life moments

The friend who can stop

You and your friend both enjoy the same game. He plays for half an hour and stops. You cannot. He is not stricter than you. He says, “I like it. I just like other things too.”

At the wedding feast

At your cousin’s wedding there is every sweet you love. Last time you ate until you felt sick. This time you take what you enjoy, eat slowly with your friends, and stop. You enjoy the whole evening.

Social media at work

You need social media for your job. A friend in the same role uses it for an hour, does the work and closes it. You learn her method: decide what you came for before you open it.

What helps

  • Enjoy things, and keep the off-switch in your own hand.
  • Decide the limit before you start, not in the middle.
  • Do not hate the things you are avoiding. Hating keeps you tied to them as well.

Try this today: Enjoy one treat today, in a fixed amount or for a fixed time, and stop when you said you would.

Bhakti thought: Krishna’s word for the peace that follows is prasada, the same word we use for food blessed by God. Peace is His gift to a mind that is not grasping.

Verse 2.65

In a peaceful mind, sorrows fade and clear thinking returns quickly.

Gem: Evenness (peace first, then answers)

प्रसादे सर्वदुःखानां हानिरस्योपजायते ।
प्रसन्नचेतसो ह्याशु बुद्धिः पर्यवतिष्ठते ॥

prasaade sarva-duhkhaanaam haanir asyopajaayate | prasanna-chetaso hy aashu buddhih paryavatishthate

“In that peace, all sorrows come to an end. The understanding of a person with a peaceful mind soon becomes steady.”

Real-life moments

The problem that solved itself

You and your friend struggle with a maths problem for an hour and grow more and more upset. She says, “Leave it. Let us get some air.” On the walk back, the answer comes to you.

After the quarrel

You have fought with your roommate, and you spend the evening rehearsing arguments. You go for a run. When you return, you can see his side too, and the conversation takes five minutes.

The decision after a good night’s sleep

At eleven at night, a work problem looks impossible, and you draft a resignation letter in your head. A friend says, “Sleep. Decide tomorrow.” In the morning it is an ordinary problem with an ordinary answer.

What helps

  • When you are stuck, calm down first and solve the problem afterwards.
  • Walk, sleep, pray or sit quietly before a hard conversation.
  • Do not trust the thoughts of an upset mind late at night.

Try this today: The next time you are stuck or upset, take a ten-minute walk before you do anything else.

Bhakti thought: Peace is not the prize at the end. It is the ground on which everything else can be done. We can ask God for it first.

Verse 2.66

Without a steady mind there is no peace, and without peace there can be no happiness.

Gem: Evenness

नास्ति बुद्धिरयुक्तस्य न चायुक्तस्य भावना ।
न चाभावयतः शान्तिरशान्तस्य कुतः सुखम् ॥

naasti buddhir ayuktasya na chaayuktasya bhaavanaa | na chaabhaavayatah shaantir ashaantasya kutah sukham

“The unsteady person has no understanding and no power to reflect. Without reflection there is no peace, and without peace, how can there be happiness?”

Real-life moments

The restless holiday

In the holidays you have every freedom: games, shows, food and friends online. Yet by evening you feel dull and irritated. Your friend says the same. You both agree that last year’s trek, with no network at all, felt far happier.

The party and the walk

At a loud college party, you keep checking your phone and comparing yourself with others. The next day you and one friend walk by the lake and talk for an hour. You go home happy and wonder why.

The busy, unhappy week

Your week is full of outings, and still you feel empty on Sunday night. A friend who spent her weekend reading and cooking seems far more rested. You ask if you can join her next Saturday.

What helps

  • When you feel dull or irritated, look for what has disturbed your peace.
  • Build some quiet into each day, even ten minutes.
  • Choose company and activities that leave you calmer, not more restless.

Try this today: Tonight, write one thing from today that left you peaceful and one that left you restless.

Bhakti thought: Krishna’s question, “How can there be happiness without peace?”, is worth carrying with us. Peace comes from keeping the mind with Him.

Verse 2.67

A mind that follows every wandering sense is carried off course, like a boat in the wind.

Gem: Mindful action (holding the rudder)

इन्द्रियाणां हि चरतां यन्मनोऽनुविधीयते ।
तदस्य हरति प्रज्ञां वायुर्नावमिवाम्भसि ॥

indriyaanaam hi charataam yan mano ’nuvidheeyate | tad asya harati prajnaam vaayur naavam ivaambhasi

“When the mind runs after the wandering senses, it carries away a person’s understanding, as the wind carries away a boat on the water.”

Real-life moments

One notification

You are writing an essay when your phone buzzes. It is only a meme from a friend. You reply, then see a video, then another. Forty minutes later you cannot remember what the essay was about.

“Let’s go, everyone is going”

You are saving money for a course. Your friends decide on a sudden trip, then a concert, then new jackets. Each time you go along. At the end of the term, the savings are gone, and so is the course.

The open office

Every conversation near your desk pulls you in. By evening you have joined ten discussions and finished nothing. A friend lends you her headphones and says, “Decide where you are going before the wind decides.”

What helps

  • Know where you are headed this hour before you begin.
  • Remove the wind. Silence notifications and move away from distraction.
  • When you notice you have drifted, come back without scolding yourself.

Try this today: Turn off notifications for one app that pulls you away most often.

Bhakti thought: A boat needs a rudder and a steady hand. When we give the rudder of the mind to God, the winds lose their power over us.

Verse 2.68

So the person whose senses are fully in hand is the one whose wisdom is steady.

Gem: Mindful action (self-control as strength)

तस्माद्यस्य महाबाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः ।
इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥

tasmaad yasya mahaa-baaho nigriheetaani sarvashah | indriyaaneendriyaarthebhyas tasya prajnaa pratishthitaa

“Therefore, mighty Arjuna, one whose senses are completely held back from their objects has steady wisdom.”

Real-life moments

The friend who says no easily

When your group pushes someone to try a cigarette behind the school, one friend just says, “No, I’m fine,” and changes the subject. Nobody teases him for long. You see that his “no” is a kind of strength, and you stand next to him.

The exam month

During exam month, your friend keeps a simple rule: phone in the warden’s office from nine to nine. People call her extreme. After the results, three of them ask her to hold them to the same rule next term.

The weekend offer

Colleagues invite you for drinks every Friday and press you when you refuse. You keep saying, cheerfully, “I will come for the food.” In time they stop pressing, and one of them quietly joins you with a lime soda.

What helps

  • Practise saying no in a friendly voice, without a long explanation.
  • Set your rule in advance, so that you are not deciding under pressure.
  • Stand beside a friend who says no. It makes it easier for both of you.

Try this today: Say a calm, friendly “no” to one small thing today that you do not really want to do.

Bhakti thought: Self-control is not a cage. It is the strength that lets us offer a whole, undivided mind to God.

Verse 2.69

A wise person is awake to what others ignore, and is not excited by what excites everyone else.

Gem: My steady Self (my own values)

या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी ।
यस्यां जाग्रति भूतानि सा निशा पश्यतो मुनेः ॥

yaa nishaa sarva-bhootaanaam tasyaam jaagarti samyamee | yasyaam jaagrati bhootaani saa nishaa pashyato muneh

“What is night for all beings is the time of waking for the self-controlled. What all beings are awake to is night for the sage who sees.”

Real-life moments

Not interested in the gossip

At lunch, your whole table is excited about who said what about whom. One friend eats quietly and then asks you how your grandmother’s operation went. You realise what she pays attention to, and what she does not.

Carnatic music and film songs

You have learned Carnatic music since you were small, and you are good at it. Hardly anyone in your class knows what it is. Everyone sings film songs, which have a huge following, and you start hiding your music class as if it were something uncool. Then you sing at a school function. The hall goes quiet, and afterwards the same friends ask, “How do you do that?” You see that what you are good at does not have to be the popular thing. Growing it is what makes you stand out.

The early morning

Your colleagues stay up late with shows and wake up tired. A friend sleeps at ten and is up at five to read and walk. They think he is missing out, and he thinks the same about them.

What helps

  • Ask what you value, not only what everyone around you is excited about.
  • It is all right to be the only one who is not interested in something.
  • Give your best hours to what matters most to you.
  • Know what you are good at and keep developing it, even when it is not the cool thing.

Try this today: Notice one thing “everyone” is excited about that you do not really care for, and let yourself skip it.

Bhakti thought: The devotee is awake to God’s presence, which the busy world sleeps through. The quiet of early morning is a good time to wake to it.

Verse 2.70

Be like the ocean. Rivers pour into it all day, yet it stays full and still.

Gem: Evenness (wide and unshaken)

आपूर्यमाणमचलप्रतिष्ठं समुद्रमापः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् ।
तद्वत्कामा यं प्रविशन्ति सर्वे स शान्तिमाप्नोति न कामकामी ॥

aapooryamaanam achala-pratishtham samudram aapah pravishanti yadvat | tadvat kaamaa yam pravishanti sarve sa shaantim aapnoti na kaama-kaamee

“Rivers flow into the ocean, which is always being filled and yet stays unmoved. In the same way, one into whom all desires flow without disturbing him attains peace, and not the one who runs after desires.”

Real-life moments

Everyone’s plans

Your phone fills with plans: a movie, a match, a birthday, a sale. Earlier you tried to do everything and ended up tired. Now you read them all, choose one and wish the others well. Your friends notice that you are calmer.

The friend who is not shaken

In the hostel, offers and temptations come to everyone alike. One friend listens, smiles and takes only what suits him. Nothing seems to push him around. You ask him about it. “I do not have to chase everything that comes,” he says.

Requests at work

Ten requests arrive before noon, and each sender calls theirs urgent. You used to run after each one. Now you take them all in, pick up one at a time and stay level. Your friends at work ask what has changed.

What helps

  • Let wants and offers arrive without jumping at each one.
  • Make your inside bigger, through sleep, prayer and time with good friends, so that small things do not flood you.
  • Choose one thing and let the rest flow past.

Try this today: The next time three things pull at you together, pause, choose one and let the other two go.

Bhakti thought: The ocean is one of the great pictures of God in our tradition. A heart that rests in Him becomes wide like the sea.

Verse 2.71

Peace comes to the one who lets go of craving, of “mine” and of “I am the one who did it.”

Gem: Same Self in all (sharing, and less “me”)

विहाय कामान्यः सर्वान्पुमांश्चरति निःस्पृहः ।
निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः स शान्तिमधिगच्छति ॥

vihaaya kaamaan yah sarvaan pumaamsh charati nihsprihah | nirmamo nirahankaarah sa shaantim adhigachchhati

“The person who gives up all cravings and lives free from longing, without the sense of ’mine’ and without ego, attains peace.”

Real-life moments

“My” best friend

Your best friend has started spending time with a new girl, and you feel she is being taken from you. You keep saying “my friend.” Then you invite the new girl to sit with both of you. Now there are three of you, and nobody has lost anyone.

Who did the most

After the college fest, your team argues about who worked hardest. You know you did a great deal. You say, “We all did it. It went well.” The argument ends, and people remember you kindly for it.

“My” idea

In a meeting, a colleague builds on your idea, and it becomes hers in everyone’s mind. You begin to say, “Actually, I...” and stop. The idea is working, and the team is better for it. You let it be.

What helps

  • Change “mine” to “ours” wherever you can. Share friends, notes and credit.
  • Let good work speak. You do not need to announce your part.
  • Notice how tight “me” and “mine” feel, and how light sharing feels.

Try this today: Share something you usually keep for yourself today, such as your notes, your snack or your friend’s company.

Bhakti thought: Everything we call “mine” was given by God and belongs to Him. Remembering this turns owning into caring.

Verse 2.72

This is the steady state. Once you reach it you are not confused again, and it is never too late to reach it.

Gem: My steady Self

एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ नैनां प्राप्य विमुह्यति ।
स्थित्वास्यामन्तकालेऽपि ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृच्छति ॥

eshaa braahmee sthitih paartha nainaam praapya vimuhyati | sthitvaasyaam anta-kaale ’pi brahma-nirvaanam richchhati

“This, Arjuna, is the state of one who rests in God. Having reached it, a person is not confused again. Staying in it, even at the end of life, one attains oneness with God.”

Real-life moments

The friend who changed

A boy in your class was known for his temper. Over two years, with a patient teacher and a few good friends, he became the calmest person in the room. When someone asks how, he says, “A little every day. I am still learning.”

Starting late

Your friend used to say, “I am not the spiritual type.” In her final year, after a hard time at home, she begins sitting quietly for ten minutes each morning. She tells you, “I wish I had started earlier. But I am glad I have started.”

Your grandmother’s calm

When you visit home from work, you notice how peaceful your grandmother is, whatever the news. You ask her if she was always like this. She laughs. “Not at your age. It came slowly, and it stayed.”

What helps

  • Go back over the four Gems and choose the one you most need now.
  • Practise a little every day. The steady state is built in small pieces.
  • Do not think it is too late or too early. Begin from where you are.

Try this today: Choose one verse from this chapter to carry with you this month, and read it every morning.

Bhakti thought: The chapter that began with Arjuna’s tears ends in peace. Krishna has shown the whole road, and He walks it with us.
